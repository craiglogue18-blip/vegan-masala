## Ingredients
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 1 tsp fennel seeds
- 1/4 tsp fenugreek seeds
- 2 green cardamom pods
- 1 small cinnamon stick
- 1 large onion, finely chopped
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- a few curry leaves
- 2 medium tomatoes, chopped
- 1 tsp paprika
- 1/4 tsp cayenne
- 1 tsp turmeric
- 1 tin chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 cup water
- 1 tin coconut milk
- 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped

## Method
1. Heat 2 tbsp neutral oil in a sauté pan over medium heat. Add 1 tsp cumin seeds, 1 tsp fennel seeds, 1/4 tsp fenugreek seeds, 2 green cardamom pods and 1 small cinnamon stick; let them sizzle for a few seconds until fragrant.
2. Add 1 large onion, finely chopped, and cook for 6 to 8 minutes, stirring often, until soft and lightly golden. Stir in 4 cloves garlic, minced, and a few curry leaves, and cook for 30 seconds more.
3. Add 2 medium tomatoes, chopped, 1 tsp paprika, 1/4 tsp cayenne and 1 tsp turmeric. Cook on medium heat, stirring regularly, until the tomatoes break down and the masala looks thick and glossy.
4. Stir in 1 tin chickpeas, drained and rinsed, 1 tsp salt and 1 cup water. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat and simmer for 10 minutes so the chickpeas take on the masala.
5. Pour in 1 tin coconut milk and warm through gently without boiling hard. Taste and adjust the seasoning if needed.
6. Finish with 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped, and serve hot.

## Notes
- Take the onion and tomato stage far enough; that is where the curry gets its body. If the masala still looks wet and pale, keep cooking before adding the chickpeas.
- Coconut milk should be warmed gently at the end. A hard boil can split it and dull the finish.
- The curry thickens as it sits. Add a splash of water when reheating if the sauce has tightened too much.
- Store in the fridge for up to 3 days in a covered container. It also freezes well for a month, though the coconut sauce may need a good stir after thawing.
