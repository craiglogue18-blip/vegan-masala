## Ingredients
- 600g/1lb 5oz potatoes, peeled and cut into chunks
- 1½ tsp salt, divided
- 4 spring onions, white and green parts finely chopped
- 2–3 small chillies (bird’s eye or similar), finely chopped, seeds in
- handful fresh coriander, finely chopped
- 2 tsp ground coriander, divided
- 1 tsp cumin seeds, toasted and crushed
- 1 tsp ginger powder
- 3 tbsp panko breadcrumbs or any other breadcrumbs
- 2 tsp sunflower oil
- 4 tsp sunflower oil
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 2 tsp finely grated fresh root ginger
- 1 tsp red chilli powder
- ½ tsp ground turmeric
- 5 tsp tomato purée
- 300g/10½oz tinned chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- handful fresh coriander, for garnish
- tomato ketchup or tamarind chutney, to serve (optional)

## Method
1. Put the 600g potatoes in a saucepan, cover with water and add ½ tsp salt. Bring to a boil and cook until the potatoes are soft through. Drain well, then leave them in a large bowl until cool enough to handle.
2. Mash the cooled potatoes, then mix in 4 spring onions, 2–3 chillies, a handful of fresh coriander, 1 tsp ground coriander, 1 tsp toasted and crushed cumin seeds, 1 tsp ginger powder and 3 tbsp breadcrumbs. Shape into 12 patties and press them lightly so they hold together.
3. Heat 4 tsp sunflower oil in a saucepan over medium heat. Add 1 tsp cumin seeds and let them sizzle, then add 2 tsp grated fresh ginger. Cook for 1 minute, stirring often, then stir in 1 tsp red chilli powder, ½ tsp turmeric, the remaining 1 tsp salt, 1 tsp ground coriander and 5 tsp tomato purée. Cook briefly, then add 100ml water and the 300g chickpeas. Simmer for 4–5 minutes, stirring now and then, until the sauce coats the chickpeas.
4. Heat 2 tsp sunflower oil in a frying pan over medium heat. Fry the patties in batches for about 3 minutes on each side, until golden and crisp. Serve hot with the chickpeas and a little fresh coriander on top.

## Notes
- Drain the potatoes well before mixing. If they are still damp, the patties can split in the pan.
- The tikki should feel firm when shaped. If the mixture is too soft, add a little more breadcrumbs, a teaspoon at a time.
- The chickpeas thicken as they sit; loosen with a splash of water when reheating.
- Leftover tikki keep well in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat in a dry frying pan or hot oven so the crust stays crisp.
- The chickpea masala can be made ahead and reheated separately. Add the tikki just before serving so they do not soften.
