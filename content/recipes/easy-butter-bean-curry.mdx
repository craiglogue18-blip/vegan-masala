## Ingredients
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 1 large onion, finely chopped
- 3 cloves garlic, crushed
- 1 inch ginger, grated
- ¼ tsp cinnamon
- Pinch ground cloves
- ¼ tsp turmeric
- ½ tsp garam masala
- ½ tbsp ground cumin
- ½ tbsp ground coriander
- 2 large tomatoes, chopped
- ½ tsp salt
- 4 cups cooked butter beans (about two 400 g tins, drained)
- 2¼ cups water
- ¼ tsp cayenne
- ¼ tsp black pepper
- 1½–3 tbsp lemon juice

## Method
1. Heat 2 tbsp neutral oil in a large pan over medium heat. Add 1 large onion, finely chopped, and cook until soft and lightly golden.
2. Stir in 3 cloves garlic, crushed, and 1 inch ginger, grated. Cook for 1 minute, stirring so the garlic does not catch.
3. Add ¼ tsp cinnamon, a pinch of ground cloves, ¼ tsp turmeric, ½ tsp garam masala, ½ tbsp ground cumin, and ½ tbsp ground coriander. Stir for 30 seconds until the spices smell warm.
4. Add 2 large tomatoes, chopped, and ½ tsp salt. Cook until the tomatoes break down and the masala turns thick and jammy.
5. Stir in 4 cups cooked butter beans, 2¼ cups water, ¼ tsp cayenne, and ¼ tsp black pepper. Bring to a boil.
6. Lower the heat and simmer for 10 to 15 minutes, stirring now and then, until the sauce thickens and coats the beans.
7. Finish with 1½–3 tbsp lemon juice. Taste and add a little more salt if needed before serving.

## Notes
- Cook the onion until it loses its raw edge and starts to turn golden; that is what gives the curry body.
- Let the tomatoes cook down properly before adding the beans. If the masala still tastes sharp, keep cooking a few minutes longer.
- The lemon juice goes in at the end so the curry stays bright. Add the lower amount first, then adjust.
- Butter beans soften the sauce as they simmer, so keep an eye on thickness and add a splash more water only if needed.
- Leftovers keep well for 3 days in the fridge and usually taste better the next day. Reheat gently with a small splash of water.
- If you use dried beans, cook them fully first; they should be tender but still hold their shape in the curry.
