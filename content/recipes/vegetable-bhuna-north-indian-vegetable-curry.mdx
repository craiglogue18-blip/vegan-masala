## Ingredients
- 1 teaspoon oil
- 1/2 teaspoon dried fenugreek leaves
- 2 cups cauliflower florets
- 1 - 2 cups other vegetables of choice, chopped
- 1/2 teaspoon salt, divided
- 2 teaspoons oil
- 1 cup chopped red onion
- 1 tablespoon ginger-garlic paste
- 1/2 - 1 teaspoon garam masala (or to taste)
- 1/4 teaspoon turmeric
- 1 teaspoon ground coriander
- 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 1/4 teaspoon salt
- 2 tablespoons tomato paste
- 1/3 cup non-dairy yogurt (or use non-dairy cream such as coconut cream or cashew cream)
- 1/2 cup or more water or non-dairy milk
- 1 cup cooked or canned (drained) chickpeas
- 1 teaspoon oil
- 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds
- 1 bay leaf
- 1/2 cup onion (chopped into 3/4 - 1-inch cubes or slices)
- 1 bell pepper (chopped into 3/4 - 1-inch slices, a mix of colored bell peppers works well)
- 1/4 teaspoon dried fenugreek leaves
- cilantro, pepper flakes, and lemon juice for garnish

## Method
1. Heat 1 teaspoon oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add 1/2 teaspoon dried fenugreek leaves, then add 2 cups cauliflower florets and 1/4 teaspoon salt. Cover and cook for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring once, until the edges start to brown.
2. Add 1 - 2 cups chopped vegetables and the remaining 1/4 teaspoon salt. If the pan looks dry, add 2 to 3 tablespoons water. Cover and cook until the cauliflower is just tender but still holds its shape.
3. Transfer the vegetables to a bowl and set aside.
4. In the same skillet, heat 2 teaspoons oil over medium heat. Add 1 cup chopped red onion and cook for 5 to 8 minutes, stirring and adding splashes of water as needed, until golden and softened.
5. Stir in 1 tablespoon ginger-garlic paste and cook for 30 seconds. Add 1/2 - 1 teaspoon garam masala, 1/4 teaspoon turmeric, 1 teaspoon ground coriander, 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika, and 1/4 teaspoon salt. Stir until the spices smell cooked.
6. Mix in 2 tablespoons tomato paste, then add 1/3 cup non-dairy yogurt and 1/2 cup or more water or non-dairy milk. Simmer until the masala turns glossy and the tomato paste loses its raw taste.
7. Return the cooked vegetables and 1 cup cooked or canned drained chickpeas to the skillet. Fold gently so the vegetables stay intact, then lower the heat.
8. In a small skillet, heat 1 teaspoon oil over medium-high heat. Add 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds and 1 bay leaf. Once the cumin darkens slightly, add 1/2 cup onion, 1 bell pepper, and 1/4 teaspoon dried fenugreek leaves. Sauté for 3 to 4 minutes, until the pepper is softened at the edges but still a little crisp.
9. Stir the tempered onion and pepper mixture into the curry. Cover, turn off the heat, and let it sit for 5 minutes before serving.
10. Finish with cilantro, pepper flakes, and a squeeze of lemon juice.

## Notes
- Root vegetables need the early cooking time in the first skillet; softer vegetables can go in with the second addition. Keep the first masala a little loose, since it thickens as it sits.
- For a thicker bhuna, use the lower end of the water range and simmer uncovered for a minute or two at the end. For a looser curry, add a splash more water or non-dairy milk when reheating.
- Leftovers keep well for up to 3 days in the fridge. Reheat gently on the stove and add a little water if the sauce has tightened too much.
- The final tempering should stay lightly crisp, not browned hard. Serve with roti, naan, or plain rice so the sauce can be scooped up properly.
