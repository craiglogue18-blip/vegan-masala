## Ingredients
- ½ cup raw basmati rice
- 6 cups oat milk
- ½ cup sugar (or substitute with date syrup, maple syrup, agave nectar, stevia, or erythritol)
- ½ tsp ground cardamom
- 2 tablespoons raisins
- 2 tablespoons sliced almonds (optional, or use pumpkin seeds instead)
- A generous pinch of saffron
- ½ tsp vanilla extract

## Method
1. Soak ½ cup raw basmati rice in a bowl with enough water to cover it by 2 to 3 inches for 1 hour. Drain well and keep the rice as it is, without rinsing away the starch.
2. Put 6 cups oat milk and the drained rice in a large saucepan. Cook over low to medium-low heat for 15 to 20 minutes, stirring every few minutes, until the rice is very soft and the milk starts to thicken.
3. Stir in ½ cup sugar and continue cooking for about 15 minutes, stirring often so the kheer does not catch on the bottom. The mixture should look creamier and slightly reduced.
4. Add ½ tsp ground cardamom, 2 tablespoons raisins, and 2 tablespoons sliced almonds. Cook for a few more minutes until the raisins plump a little and the rice looks swollen and tender.
5. Stir in a generous pinch of saffron and cook for 5 minutes more, letting the colour and aroma move through the pudding.
6. Take the pan off the heat and stir in ½ tsp vanilla extract. Cover the kheer and let it cool to room temperature before serving, so it settles to the right texture.
7. Serve chilled or at room temperature.

## Notes
- Kheer thickens as it cools. If it becomes too firm, loosen it with a splash of oat milk while reheating on low heat.
- Keep leftovers covered in the refrigerator for up to 3 days. Stir before serving, since the rice settles and the surface may look thicker.
- The sweetness can be adjusted to taste, especially if using date syrup or another liquid sweetener. Add it gradually and stop when the balance feels right.
- For a little more texture at the table, top each bowl with extra almonds or pumpkin seeds just before serving.
