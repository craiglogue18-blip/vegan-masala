## Ingredients
- 2 cups atta flour (or whole wheat flour), plus extra for dusting
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 3/4-1 cup water

## Method
1. In a large mixing bowl, combine 2 cups atta flour and 1 teaspoon salt.
2. Gradually add 3/4 cup water, mixing with a spatula or your hands until a dough starts to come together. Add a little more water only if the flour still looks dry.
3. Knead the dough for a few minutes until smooth and elastic. If it feels sticky, dust with a little more atta flour as you work.
4. Cover the dough with a damp cloth and rest for 25 to 30 minutes.
5. Knead the rested dough briefly, then divide it into 8 equal portions.
6. Shape one portion into a smooth ball and flatten it lightly with your palm.
7. Dust the work surface and rolling pin with atta flour, then roll the dough into an oval. Turn it 90 degrees and roll again into a circle about 5 to 6 inches across.
8. Repeat with the remaining portions, dusting lightly as needed so the dough does not stick.
9. Heat a cast-iron or non-stick pan over medium-high heat until hot.
10. Place one chapati in the pan and cook for 1 to 2 minutes, until small bubbles appear and the surface starts to lift. Flip and cook the second side for about 1 minute, until light golden spots appear.
11. Cook the remaining chapatis the same way and serve them hot. If you like, finish each one over an open flame for a few seconds to encourage puffing.

## Notes
- Use atta flour if you can; it gives chapati a softer, more flexible texture than standard whole wheat flour.
- The dough should feel soft, not stiff. If it is too firm, the chapatis will crack at the edges when rolled.
- A well-heated pan matters. If the pan is too cool, the chapati dries out before it cooks through.
- Keep cooked chapatis wrapped in a clean kitchen towel so they stay supple while you finish the batch.
- Leftovers keep in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat on a hot pan for a few seconds on each side, or wrap briefly in foil to warm through.
