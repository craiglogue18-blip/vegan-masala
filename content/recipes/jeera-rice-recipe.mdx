## Ingredients
- 1 tsp oil
- 2 teaspoons cumin seeds
- 2 bay leaves
- 4 green cardamom pods
- 4 cloves
- 2 cups raw basmati rice
- 4 cups water, at room temperature
- Salt to taste
- A generous pinch of saffron

## Method
1. Heat 1 tsp oil in a skillet over medium heat. Add 2 teaspoons cumin seeds, 2 bay leaves, 4 green cardamom pods and 4 cloves, and let them sizzle until fragrant.
2. Stir in 2 cups raw basmati rice and cook for about 1 minute, just until the grains look opaque at the edges.
3. Pour in 4 cups water and add salt to taste; it should taste a little saltier than you want the finished rice. Add a generous pinch of saffron and bring to a boil over medium-high heat.
4. As the rice cooks, stir once or twice so it does not catch. When most of the water has been absorbed, cover the pot tightly, lower the heat to the smallest flame and cook for 15 minutes.
5. Turn off the heat and leave the rice covered for 10 minutes. Fluff gently with a fork before serving so the grains stay separate.

## Notes
- If you want a slightly deeper base, toast the rice in the oil for a minute before adding the water, but do not let it brown.
- The rice should be tender with distinct grains, not sticky. If it still feels firm after resting, cover it for a few more minutes over very low heat.
- Leftovers keep well in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat with a splash of water in a covered pan over low heat, or steam it gently in the microwave.
- Brown rice can be used, but it needs a longer cook and more water; the timing will not match basmati.
- Keep the seasoning clean and balanced. Jeera rice should carry the cumin clearly without turning heavy.
