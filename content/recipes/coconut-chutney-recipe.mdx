## Ingredients
- ½ cup fresh grated coconut (tightly packed) or ½ cup desiccated coconut
- 1 or 2 green chilies (or ½ teaspoon chopped green chilies or serrano or Anaheim pepper)
- ½ inch ginger (optional, or substitute with 1 to 2 small garlic cloves)
- 2 tablespoons roasted chana dal (optional)
- salt (to taste)
- 3 to 4 tablespoons water (or as needed for grinding)
- ½ tablespoon oil (sesame, peanut, coconut, sunflower, or any neutral oil)
- ½ teaspoon mustard seeds
- ½ teaspoon urad dal (split and husked black gram)
- ½ teaspoon cumin seeds (optional)
- 9 to 10 curry leaves (or 1 sprig of curry leaves)
- 1 pinch asafoetida (hing)
- 1 dry red chili (broken with seeds removed)

## Method
1. In a chutney grinder or small blender jar, combine ½ cup fresh grated coconut, 1 or 2 green chilies, and ½ inch ginger.
2. Add 2 tablespoons roasted chana dal and salt to taste, then pour in 3 to 4 tablespoons water.
3. Grind to a smooth or slightly coarse chutney, adding a little more water only if needed.
4. Transfer the chutney to a serving bowl.
5. Heat ½ tablespoon oil in a small pan over medium heat.
6. Add ½ teaspoon mustard seeds and let them start to splutter.
7. Stir in ½ teaspoon urad dal and ½ teaspoon cumin seeds, if using, and fry until the urad dal turns light golden.
8. Add 9 to 10 curry leaves, 1 dry red chili, and 1 pinch asafoetida.
9. Fry for a few seconds, just until the curry leaves crisp and the spices smell fragrant; do not let the red chili darken.
10. Pour the tempering over the chutney and mix well before serving.

## Notes
- This chutney should be spoonable, not runny. Start with the lower amount of water and add more only if the grinder needs it.
- Fresh coconut gives a softer, cleaner taste; desiccated coconut works too, but the chutney will be a little less delicate.
- The roasted chana dal helps the chutney hold body and gives a mild nutty note. It can be left out, but the chutney will be thinner.
- Keep leftovers in the fridge for up to 3 days in a covered container. Stir before serving, since the chutney may thicken after chilling.
- If serving later, add a spoon or two of water and adjust salt after refrigeration. The tempering can be made fresh if you want the curry leaves crisp again.
