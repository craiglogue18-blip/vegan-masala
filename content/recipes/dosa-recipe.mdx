## Ingredients
- 2 cups rice (white basmati rice recommended)
- ½ cup urad dal (black gram dal)
- 2 tablespoons chana dal (Bengal gram dal)
- ½ teaspoon fenugreek seeds (methi seeds)
- ½ cup poha (flattened rice)
- 1½ cups water, divided
- Salt to taste
- Vegetable oil or cooking spray for greasing the griddle

## Method
1. In a large bowl, combine 2 cups rice, ½ cup urad dal, 2 tablespoons chana dal, ½ teaspoon fenugreek seeds, and ½ cup poha. Cover with water by 2 to 3 inches and soak for 8 hours.
2. Drain the soaked mixture and transfer it to a blender. Add 1 cup of the 1½ cups water and blend until smooth with a slight fine grain from the rice.
3. Check the batter; it should be thick but pourable. Add the remaining ½ cup water little by little only if needed to loosen it.
4. Transfer the batter to a large bowl, cover, and leave it in a warm place for at least 8 hours or overnight, until it looks puffy and lightly risen.
5. Stir in salt to taste after fermentation. The batter should fall from the spoon in a steady ribbon; if it feels too tight, stir in a small splash of water.
6. Heat a griddle over medium-high heat until a few drops of water sizzle and disappear within 5 seconds.
7. Pour a ladleful of batter in the centre of the griddle and spread it quickly in a thin spiral with the base of the ladle.
8. Drizzle a little vegetable oil or use cooking spray around the edges. If using spray, lower the heat briefly before spraying.
9. Cook until the edges turn lacy and golden and the surface is dry. Ease the dosa off with a flat spatula and serve at once.
10. Repeat with the remaining batter, stirring the batter lightly between dosas if it settles.

## Notes
- Fermentation changes with room temperature. In a warm kitchen, the batter may be ready sooner; in a cool one, give it more time until it looks airy and smells mildly sour.
- If the batter becomes too thick after fermenting, loosen it with a tablespoon of water at a time. If it turns too thin, a little rice flour can help bring it back, but add only enough to keep the batter spreadable.
- For dosas with more colour and crispness, make sure the griddle is properly heated before pouring the batter. If the pan is too cool, the batter will stick and spread unevenly.
- Leftover batter keeps well in the fridge for up to 2 days. Stir it gently before cooking and bring it back to room temperature if possible.
- Cooked dosas are best eaten straight away. If needed, reheat them on a hot griddle rather than in the microwave so the edges stay crisp.
