## Ingredients
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 160 g plant-based chick'n (any brand)
- 1 white onion, finely chopped
- 4 garlic cloves, finely chopped
- A thumb-sized piece of ginger, finely chopped
- 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds
- 1/2 teaspoon coriander seeds
- 4 cardamom pods
- 1 tbsp garam masala
- 2 tsp ground turmeric
- 200ml rich coconut milk
- 200ml water
- 10g desiccated coconut
- A small handful of coriander, finely chopped
- 1 tbsp sugar
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper

## Method
1. In a pestle and mortar, crush together 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds, 1/2 teaspoon coriander seeds, and the seeds from 4 cardamom pods. Set aside.
2. Heat 2 tablespoons of neutral oil in a large frying pan over medium heat and fry 160g of chick'n for 3-4 minutes until golden on all sides. Remove and set aside.
3. Add 1 finely chopped onion to the same pan and sauté for about 6 minutes, until soft and beginning to gain some colour. Stir in 4 chopped garlic cloves and the chopped ginger, cooking for 1 minute until fragrant. Add 1 tbsp garam masala and 2 tsp turmeric, then stir for 30 seconds.
4. Return the chick'n to the pan along with the spice mix, then pour in 200ml coconut milk, 200ml water, and 1 tbsp sugar. Simmer over low heat for 10 minutes, stirring regularly until the sauce thickens and becomes glossy.
5. Stir in 10g of desiccated coconut and a small handful of chopped coriander. Season generously with salt and pepper, then turn off the heat and let the korma stand for 10 minutes before serving.

## Notes
- The korma should have a smooth, creamy texture. If it's too thick, add a splash more water to reach your desired consistency.
- Leftovers can be refrigerated for up to 3 days. Reheat gently on the stovetop, adding a little water if needed.
- This dish pairs well with rice, naan, or rice poppadoms, making for a versatile meal.
