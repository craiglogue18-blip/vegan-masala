## Ingredients
- 2 tbsp oil
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 large onion, finely chopped
- 1 inch ginger, grated
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp garam masala
- 1 red chilli, finely chopped
- 200 ml tomato puree
- 1 tin chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tin coconut milk
- 200 g spinach
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped

## Method
1. Set the Instant Pot to sauté and heat 2 tbsp oil.
2. Add 1 tsp cumin seeds and 1 bay leaf; let them sizzle for a few seconds.
3. Stir in 1 large onion, finely chopped, and cook until the edges turn golden.
4. Add 1 inch ginger, grated, 4 cloves garlic, minced, and 1 red chilli, finely chopped; cook for a minute until fragrant.
5. Mix in 1 tsp turmeric and 1 tsp garam masala, then add 200 ml tomato puree. Cook until the masala looks thicker and the raw tomato smell has cooked off.
6. Add 1 tin chickpeas, drained and rinsed, 1 tsp salt, and 1 tin coconut milk. Stir well, making sure the chickpeas are coated.
7. Seal the Instant Pot and pressure cook on high for 3 minutes.
8. Leave it for 5 minutes on natural release, then release the remaining pressure.
9. Stir in 200 g spinach and cook until wilted.
10. Finish with 1 tbsp lemon juice and 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped. Add a short sauté if you want the curry a little thicker.
11. Serve hot.

## Notes
- Take time with the onion and tomato base; that is where the curry gets its body. If the masala still tastes sharp before pressure cooking, give it another minute on sauté.
- Coconut milk softens the spices, so salt and lemon should be adjusted at the end, after pressure cooking.
- If you want a looser curry, leave it as it is. For a thicker finish, simmer briefly on sauté with the lid off.
- Leftovers keep well for up to 3 days in the fridge. Reheat gently so the coconut milk stays smooth.
- The spinach should go in at the end so it stays green and tender instead of turning dull.
