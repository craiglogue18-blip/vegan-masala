## Ingredients
- 150g dried soya mince (or use 400g fresh or frozen plant-based mince)
- 3 tbsp oil, divided
- 2 large onions, finely diced
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tsp sugar
- 4 large cloves garlic, crushed
- 1 tbsp grated ginger
- 2 dried bay leaves (optional)
- 2 tsp smoked paprika (hot or mild)
- 2 tsp ground cumin
- 2 tsp ground coriander
- 1/2 tsp ground turmeric
- 1 x 400g can chopped tomatoes or passata
- 1 tbsp dark soy sauce (or tamari for a gluten-free option)
- 50ml oat cream (or milk for a lighter option)
- 1 1/2 tsp cornflour (cornstarch)
- 250ml water
- 1 tbsp garam masala
- 225g frozen peas
- 2 tbsp chopped coriander leaves
- 1 tbsp chopped mint leaves
- Chopped green chillies, mint and fried onions (garnish, optional)
- Plant-based butter (to serve, optional)

## Method
1. Put 150g dried soya mince in a bowl and cover with hot water. Leave for 10 minutes, then drain well in a fine sieve, rinse under cold water, and squeeze out excess moisture. If using 400g fresh or frozen plant-based mince, skip this step.
2. Heat 2 tbsp of the oil in a large, heavy-based pan over medium heat. Add the 2 large finely diced onions with 1 tsp salt and 1 tsp sugar, and cook for about 10 minutes until deep golden and soft.
3. Stir in 4 crushed garlic cloves, 1 tbsp grated ginger, and 2 dried bay leaves, if using. Cook for 1 to 2 minutes until the raw aroma fades.
4. Add the drained soya mince and cook over high heat for 5 to 6 minutes, stirring often, until lightly browned with a few crisp edges. Remove from the pan and set aside.
5. In a small bowl, whisk 50ml oat cream with 1 1/2 tsp cornflour until smooth and lump-free.
6. Return the pan to medium-low heat and add the remaining 1 tbsp oil. Stir in 2 tsp smoked paprika, 2 tsp ground cumin, 2 tsp ground coriander, and 1/2 tsp ground turmeric. Bloom the spices for about 15 seconds, just until fragrant.
7. Add 1 x 400g can chopped tomatoes or passata and 1 tbsp dark soy sauce. Cook for a minute or two, scraping the base of the pan.
8. Stir in the browned soya mince, the oat cream and cornflour mixture, 250ml water, and 1 tbsp garam masala. Mix well, then lower the heat to a gentle simmer.
9. Cover and cook for 20 minutes, stirring now and then, until the masala has thickened and the tomato base has cooked down. If you want a drier keema, leave the pan uncovered for the last few minutes.
10. Stir in 225g frozen peas, 2 tbsp chopped coriander leaves, and 1 tbsp chopped mint leaves for the final 5 minutes. Add the chopped green chillies only if you want extra heat.
11. Taste and adjust salt if needed. Serve hot with the optional fried onions, extra herbs, and a little plant-based butter on top, if using.

## Notes
- The curry thickens as it sits, so loosen it with a splash of water when reheating if needed. Warm it gently on the stove rather than boiling hard, which can make the mince turn dry.
- If you use fresh or frozen plant-based mince, cook it long enough for some colour on the edges before adding the tomatoes. That extra browning gives the curry better body.
- Leftovers keep well in the fridge for 3 days and freeze for up to 1 month. Defrost fully before reheating so the texture stays even.
- The final texture can be adjusted to suit the table: cook a little longer for a drier keema, or stop earlier for a softer, saucier finish.
