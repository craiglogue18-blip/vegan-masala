## Ingredients
- ½ cup tamarind (imli, tightly packed, preferably without seeds)
- 1.75 cups water (or as needed)
- 1 teaspoon oil (any neutral-tasting oil)
- ½ teaspoon cumin seeds
- ½ teaspoon ground ginger powder (saunth powder)
- 1 pinch asafoetida (hing)
- ¼ teaspoon red chili powder (or cayenne pepper)
- ½ cup chopped jaggery (adjust to taste)
- Salt (to taste)

## Method
1. Put ½ cup tamarind in 1.75 cups water and soak for 4 to 5 hours, or overnight, in a small bowl or pan.
2. When the tamarind is soft, squeeze it well in the soaking water to release the pulp, then remove the seeds and fibres.
3. Strain the tamarind liquid if you want a smoother chutney, and keep it aside.
4. Heat 1 teaspoon oil in a small frying pan over medium heat.
5. Add ½ teaspoon cumin seeds and let them crackle.
6. Lower the heat and stir in ½ teaspoon ground ginger powder, 1 pinch asafoetida and ¼ teaspoon red chili powder.
7. Add the strained tamarind pulp and cook for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring so it does not stick to the pan.
8. Add ½ cup chopped jaggery and salt to taste, then simmer for 4 to 5 minutes until the jaggery dissolves and the chutney thickens slightly.
9. Cool completely before transferring to an airtight container.

## Notes
- The chutney should be pourable and glossy when you take it off the heat; it thickens more as it cools. If it reduces too far, stir in a little warm water.
- Taste after the jaggery has melted. Tamarind varies in sharpness, so a small extra pinch of salt can steady the balance.
- For chaat or drizzling, strain the chutney well. For samosas and pakoras, a slightly rustic texture works fine.
- Keep it in the fridge for up to 2 weeks. It can be served cold, or loosened with a spoonful of warm water if it thickens in storage.
