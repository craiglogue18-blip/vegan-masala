## Ingredients
- 2 tbsp oil
- 1 tsp mustard seeds
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 1 small cinnamon stick
- 2 red onions, diced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 inch ginger, grated
- 1 tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp paprika
- 1 tsp garam masala
- 1 tbsp vinegar
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tsp sugar
- red pepper flakes, to taste
- 200 ml passata or smooth tomato purée
- 1 small cauliflower, cut into florets
- 3 medium potatoes, cubed
- 200 g mushrooms, sliced
- 500 ml hot water
- 200 g firm tofu, cubed
- 200 g vegan sausage, sliced
- 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped

## Method
1. Heat 2 tbsp oil in a heavy pan over medium heat. Add 1 tsp mustard seeds, 1 tsp cumin seeds and 1 small cinnamon stick, and let them sizzle for a few seconds.
2. Add 2 red onions, diced, and cook, stirring now and then, until they turn deep golden at the edges and soften fully.
3. Stir in 4 cloves garlic, minced, and 1 inch ginger, grated. Cook for 1 minute, then add 1 tsp turmeric, 1 tsp paprika, 1 tsp garam masala, 1 tbsp vinegar, 1 tsp salt, 1 tsp sugar and red pepper flakes, to taste.
4. Add 200 ml passata or smooth tomato purée and cook until the masala darkens and the oil begins to show at the edges of the pan.
5. Add 1 small cauliflower, cut into florets, 3 medium potatoes, cubed, and 200 g mushrooms, sliced. Stir well to coat, then pour in 500 ml hot water.
6. Cover and simmer for 20 to 30 minutes, stirring now and then, until the potatoes are tender and the sauce has reduced to a thick, spoonable consistency.
7. Add 200 g firm tofu, cubed, and 200 g vegan sausage, sliced. Simmer for 5 minutes so they absorb the masala without breaking apart.
8. Turn off the heat and finish with 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped. Taste and adjust salt or vinegar if needed before serving.

## Notes
- Take the onions well past softening; the masala needs that deeper base so the tomato doesn’t stay sharp in the wrong way.
- A vindaloo-style curry should be bold and tangy, but not harsh. If the vinegar feels too strong after simmering, balance it with a pinch more sugar or a little salt.
- The sauce should cling to the vegetables. If it gets too tight before the potatoes are cooked, add a splash of hot water and keep simmering.
- For cleaner texture, add the tofu near the end as written. It stays firmer and holds the masala better than if it cooks for too long.
- Leftovers keep well in the fridge for up to 3 days and often taste better the next day once the spices have settled.
- Reheat gently with a spoonful of water, especially if the potatoes have absorbed a lot of the sauce.
