## Ingredients
- 2 tbsp vegetable oil
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 2 medium onions, chopped
- 1 green chilli, finely chopped
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 3 medium potatoes, cubed
- 2 medium eggplants, sliced lengthwise
- 1 tsp salt, divided
- 1 tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp garam masala
- 2 tsp coriander powder
- 1 red chilli, finely chopped
- 2 medium tomatoes, chopped
- 1 cup water
- 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped

## Method
1. Keep 3 medium cubed potatoes in water until needed. Soak 2 medium eggplants, sliced lengthwise, in water with a pinch of the 1 tsp salt, then drain before cooking.
2. Heat 2 tbsp vegetable oil in a wide pan over medium heat. Add 1 tsp cumin seeds and let them crackle.
3. Add 2 medium chopped onions and 1 green chilli, finely chopped. Cook until the onions soften and turn light golden at the edges.
4. Stir in 4 cloves garlic, minced, and cook for 30 to 40 seconds, just until the raw smell fades.
5. Drain the potatoes and add them to the pan. Cook for about 3 minutes, stirring now and then, until the edges begin to turn opaque.
6. Add 2 medium eggplants, sliced lengthwise, and turn gently through the onions so they start to soften without breaking up.
7. Add 1 tsp turmeric, 1 tsp garam masala, 2 tsp coriander powder, the remaining salt from the 1 tsp salt, and 1 red chilli, finely chopped. Mix well so the vegetables are evenly coated.
8. Add 2 medium tomatoes, chopped, and cook until they soften, break down, and the masala looks thick rather than watery.
9. Pour in 1 cup water, cover, and simmer until the potatoes are tender and the eggplant is soft. If the pan dries out too quickly, add a small splash of hot water.
10. Uncover and cook for a few minutes more if needed, until the sauce sits around the vegetables and no longer looks loose. Finish with 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped, and serve hot.

## Notes
- Cook the onions properly before adding the potatoes; this builds the body of the curry and keeps the spices from tasting raw.
- Eggplant softens quickly, so stir with a light hand once it goes in. Too much stirring will break the slices.
- If you want a drier aloo baingan, cook uncovered at the end until the masala clings to the vegetables. For a looser curry, add a little extra hot water.
- Use potatoes cut to a similar size so they cook through at the same rate as the eggplant.
- Leftovers keep well in the fridge for up to 3 days and usually taste even better the next day. Reheat with a spoonful of water over low heat.
- If the masala catches on the pan, lower the heat and loosen it with a splash of water rather than letting it scorch.
