## Ingredients
- ½ cup fresh grated coconut (tightly packed) or ½ cup desiccated coconut
- 1 or 2 green chilies (or ½ teaspoon chopped green chilies or serrano or Anaheim pepper)
- ½ inch ginger (optional, or substitute with 1 to 2 small garlic cloves)
- 2 tablespoons roasted chana dal (optional)
- salt, to taste
- 3 to 4 tablespoons water, plus more if needed for grinding
- ½ tablespoon oil (sesame, peanut, coconut, sunflower, or any neutral oil)
- ½ teaspoon mustard seeds
- ½ teaspoon urad dal (split and husked black gram)
- ½ teaspoon cumin seeds (optional)
- 9 to 10 curry leaves (or 1 sprig of curry leaves)
- 1 pinch asafoetida (hing)
- 1 dry red chili, broken and seeds removed

## Method
1. In a chutney grinder or small blender jar, add ½ cup fresh grated coconut, 1 or 2 green chilies, ½ inch ginger, 2 tablespoons roasted chana dal, and salt to taste.
2. Pour in 3 to 4 tablespoons water and grind to a smooth or slightly coarse chutney. Add a little more water only if the blades need help.
3. Transfer the chutney to a serving bowl and keep it aside while you make the tempering.
4. Heat ½ tablespoon oil in a small pan over medium heat.
5. Add ½ teaspoon mustard seeds and let them start to crackle.
6. Stir in ½ teaspoon urad dal and ½ teaspoon cumin seeds, if using, and fry until the urad dal turns light golden.
7. Add 9 to 10 curry leaves, 1 dry red chili, and 1 pinch asafoetida.
8. Fry for a few seconds, just until the curry leaves crisp and the spices smell fragrant; do not let the red chili darken.
9. Pour the hot tempering over the chutney and mix well before serving.

## Notes
- Keep the chutney spoonable rather than thin. Fresh coconut usually needs a little less water than desiccated coconut.
- Roasted chana dal gives body and helps the chutney hold well beside dosa or idli. Leave it out if you need a lighter chutney, but the texture will be looser.
- If the chutney sits for a while, it may thicken. Stir in a spoonful of water and a little more salt before serving.
- Leftovers keep in the fridge for up to 3 days in a covered container. The tempering softens on standing, so make a fresh one if you want the curry leaves crisp again.
- For a sharper chutney, use the full amount of green chilli. For a milder one, reduce it and keep the ginger in for warmth.
