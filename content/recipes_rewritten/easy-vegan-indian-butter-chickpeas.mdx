## Ingredients
- 1 cup red onions, fine dice
- 1 Tablespoon minced garlic
- 1 Tablespoon minced ginger
- 2 Tablespoons tomato paste
- 1 teaspoon garlic powder
- 1 teaspoon onion powder
- 2 Tablespoons dried minced onions
- 1 Tablespoon + 1 teaspoon curry powder
- 1 teaspoon garam masala
- ½ teaspoon smoked paprika
- 1/8 teaspoon cayenne pepper (+/-)
- 1 cup vegetable broth
- 1 can (14 oz) petite diced tomatoes
- 2 Tablespoons reduced sodium tamari
- ¼ cup + 1 Tablespoon unsweetened, unsalted peanut butter
- 2 cans (15 oz each) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 1 cup light coconut milk
- 1 ½ cups soy curls
- Rice, potatoes, or quinoa for serving
- Sea salt, to taste

## Method
1. If using 1 ½ cups soy curls, prepare them according to the package directions, then drain well and squeeze out excess water. Set aside.
2. In a small bowl, mix 1 teaspoon garlic powder, 1 teaspoon onion powder, 2 Tablespoons dried minced onions, 1 Tablespoon + 1 teaspoon curry powder, 1 teaspoon garam masala, ½ teaspoon smoked paprika, and 1/8 teaspoon cayenne pepper (+/-).
3. Heat 1 cup red onions in a large deep skillet or Dutch oven over medium-high heat for 7 to 9 minutes, stirring often, until softened and starting to turn golden at the edges.
4. Lower the heat to medium. Add 1 Tablespoon minced garlic, 1 Tablespoon minced ginger, and 2 Tablespoons tomato paste. Stir constantly for 1 minute, then add the spice mix and cook for 1 minute more so the spices bloom in the oil and onion base.
5. Pour in 1 cup vegetable broth, 1 can (14 oz) petite diced tomatoes, and 2 Tablespoons reduced sodium tamari. Scrape up any bits from the bottom of the pan and bring the mixture to a boil, then reduce to a steady simmer.
6. Stir in 2 cans (15 oz each) chickpeas, drained and rinsed, and simmer on low for 5 minutes so the chickpeas take on the masala.
7. Add ¼ cup + 1 Tablespoon unsweetened, unsalted peanut butter and 1 cup light coconut milk. Stir until the sauce is smooth, then add the soy curls if using. Simmer for 5 minutes more, stirring now and then, until the sauce looks glossy and slightly thickened.
8. Taste and season with sea salt, to taste. Adjust the cayenne if you want more heat, then serve hot.

## Notes
- The sauce should coat the chickpeas without turning stiff. If it reduces too far, loosen it with a splash of vegetable broth or water.
- Peanut butter gives body here, so use an unsweetened, unsalted one. Sweetened peanut butter will throw off the balance.
- Leftovers keep well in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat gently so the coconut milk stays smooth; add a little water if the sauce has tightened.
- The chickpeas and soy curls both hold up well, but the soy curls should be squeezed dry before adding or they can water down the masala.
- For a milder pan, keep the cayenne light. For a deeper, warmer heat, add a little more when you finish the sauce.
