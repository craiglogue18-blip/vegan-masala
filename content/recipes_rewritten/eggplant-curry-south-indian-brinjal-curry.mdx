## Ingredients
- 1 medium aubergine, chopped
- 1 tsp salt
- 1/2 tsp black pepper
- 2 tbsp oil, divided
- 1 tsp mustard seeds
- a handful of curry leaves
- 1 large onion, finely chopped
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 inch piece of ginger, grated
- 2 medium tomatoes, chopped
- 2 green cardamom pods
- 1/4 tsp ground cloves
- 1 tsp chilli powder
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1 cup water
- 1 tin coconut milk
- 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped

## Method
1. Heat the oven to 240°C (220°C fan). Toss the chopped aubergine with 1 tbsp oil, the salt and black pepper.
2. Spread the aubergine on a tray and roast for about 30 minutes, until soft and browned at the edges.
3. Heat the remaining 1 tbsp oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the mustard seeds and let them pop.
4. Add the curry leaves and stir for a few seconds until they turn crisp and fragrant.
5. Add the onion and cook slowly, stirring now and then, until golden brown and well softened.
6. Stir in the garlic, ginger, tomatoes, cardamom pods, ground cloves, chilli powder and cumin. Cook until the tomatoes break down and the masala looks thick, darkened and no longer raw.
7. Add the water and roasted aubergine. Simmer gently for 20 to 25 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the curry has body and the aubergine is very soft.
8. Pour in the coconut milk and warm through gently without letting it boil hard.
9. Finish with the coriander and check the seasoning before serving.

## Notes
- Take time with the onions; a proper golden colour gives the curry its depth. Pale onions leave the gravy flat.
- Cook the tomato masala until the oil begins to separate at the edges and the raw smell has gone. That step gives the curry a steadier base.
- The roasted aubergine breaks down into the gravy and thickens it naturally, so there is no need for flour or starch.
- Keep the coconut milk at a gentle heat. If it boils hard, the gravy can split and lose its smooth finish.
- Leftovers keep well for up to 3 days in the fridge. The curry usually settles and tastes even better the next day.
- If the curry thickens after chilling, loosen it with a splash of water while reheating over low heat.
