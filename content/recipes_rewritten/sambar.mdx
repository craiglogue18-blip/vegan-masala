## Ingredients
- 1.5 litres/2½ pints water
- 100g/3½oz toor dal (yellow split pigeon peas), well rinsed
- 1 small onion, chopped
- 100g/3½oz carrots, cut into 2cm/¾in chunks
- 100g/3½oz pumpkin or butternut squash, peeled and cut into 2cm/¾in chunks
- 100g/3½oz green beans, cut into 2cm/¾in lengths
- 1 medium tomato, chopped
- 1½ tsp ground turmeric
- 1½ tsp sugar
- 1½ tsp salt
- 50ml/2fl oz vegetable oil
- 1 tsp chana dal
- 1 tsp fenugreek seeds
- 1 tsp coriander seeds
- 4 dried Kashmiri chillies with seeds
- handful fresh curry leaves
- 1 tsp asafoetida
- 2 tbsp vegetable oil
- 1 small onion, finely chopped
- 1 tsp black mustard seeds
- 1½ tsp fenugreek seeds

## Method
1. Put 1.5 litres/2½ pints of water into a large deep pan and bring it to a boil. Add 100g/3½oz well-rinsed toor dal, 1 chopped small onion, 100g/3½oz carrots, 100g/3½oz pumpkin or butternut squash, 100g/3½oz green beans and 1 chopped medium tomato. Lower the heat and simmer uncovered for 30 minutes.
2. Stir in 1½ tsp ground turmeric, 1½ tsp sugar and 1½ tsp salt. Continue to simmer gently for about 15 minutes, until the dal is soft and the vegetables are tender.
3. For the masala, heat 50ml/2fl oz vegetable oil in a frying pan over medium heat. Add 1 tsp chana dal, 1 tsp fenugreek seeds, 1 tsp coriander seeds, 4 dried Kashmiri chillies, a handful of fresh curry leaves and 1 tsp asafoetida. Fry for 30 to 60 seconds, just until fragrant, then transfer to a spice grinder and blend to a paste.
4. Stir the ground masala into the simmering dal and vegetables. Simmer for 5 minutes so the raw spice taste cooks out and the sambar thickens slightly.
5. For the tarka, heat 2 tbsp vegetable oil in a clean frying pan over medium heat. Add 1 small finely chopped onion, 1 tsp black mustard seeds and 1½ tsp fenugreek seeds. Cook for about 10 minutes, stirring often, until the onion is soft and golden-brown.
6. Pour the tarka into the sambar and stir through well. Taste and adjust salt if needed, then serve hot.

## Notes
- Sambar should pour like a loose stew, not sit like a thick curry. If it tightens on standing, loosen it with a little hot water when reheating.
- The dal should be fully soft by the end. If it is still holding shape, give it a little longer before adding the masala.
- Leftovers keep well in the fridge for up to 3 days and usually taste better the next day. Reheat gently over low heat so the vegetables stay intact.
- If you want a sharper sambar, add a splash of water after reheating and check the salt again. The tomato and spice base will carry it.
- Serve with rice, dosa or idli. A little coconut chutney on the side fits the texture and the heat very well.
