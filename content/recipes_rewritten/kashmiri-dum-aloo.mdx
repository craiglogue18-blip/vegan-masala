## Ingredients
- 60g almonds, soaked
- 1 inch ginger, grated
- 1 tsp garam masala
- 1/4 tsp cayenne pepper
- 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped
- 1 tsp sugar
- 2 tbsp oil
- 1 tsp chilli powder
- 1/2 tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp salt
- 3 medium potatoes, cubed
- 1 tin coconut milk, divided
- 1 tbsp lemon juice

## Method
1. Blend the soaked 60g almonds, 1 inch ginger, grated, 1 tsp garam masala, 1/4 tsp cayenne pepper, 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped, 1 tsp sugar and a splash of water to a smooth paste.
2. Toss 3 medium potatoes, cubed, with 2 tbsp oil, 1 tsp chilli powder, 1/2 tsp turmeric and 1 tsp salt until evenly coated.
3. Spread the potatoes on a tray and roast at 200°C for about 30 minutes, turning once, until tender at the edges and lightly browned.
4. Scrape the almond paste into a saucepan and bring it to a gentle simmer over low heat.
5. Stir in half of the 1 tin coconut milk, then add the roasted potatoes and turn them through the sauce.
6. Cover and cook on low for 15–20 minutes, stirring once or twice, until the masala thickens and coats the potatoes.
7. Add the remaining coconut milk and 1 tbsp lemon juice, then simmer for 2 minutes more.
8. Taste and adjust the seasoning, then serve hot.

## Notes
- Roasting gives the potatoes better shape in the sauce. If you boil them instead, stop as soon as they are just tender, otherwise they will break down too much.
- Keep the almond paste at a low simmer. If it catches on the base, add a spoonful of water and lower the heat rather than letting it fry hard.
- Adding the coconut milk in two stages keeps the sauce smooth and rich without turning it heavy.
- This curry keeps well for 3 days in the fridge. Reheat gently with a splash of water to loosen the sauce.
- The flavour deepens overnight, and the potatoes take on more of the masala by the next day.
