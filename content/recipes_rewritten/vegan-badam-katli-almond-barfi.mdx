## Ingredients
- 1¼ cups sugar
- ½ cup water
- 1 tsp rose water (optional)
- ½ tsp saffron (optional)
- 1 tsp ground cardamom
- 2 cups blanched almond flour
- 1 tsp cornstarch
- 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
- 1 tbsp avocado oil, plus more for oiling your hands and the surface

## Method
1. Set a heavy-bottomed pot over medium heat. Add 1¼ cups sugar, ½ cup water and 1 tsp rose water, if using, and stir just until the sugar dissolves and the syrup turns clear.
2. Add ½ tsp saffron and 1 tsp ground cardamom. Bring the syrup to a boil, then lower the heat and simmer for about 5 minutes, stirring often, until it looks slightly thickened.
3. Check for one-string consistency by lifting a little syrup from the pot and letting it cool for a moment. Press it between your thumb and forefinger; it should form a single thin thread.
4. Add 2 cups blanched almond flour and 1 tsp cornstarch all at once. Stir firmly for 2 to 3 minutes until the mixture thickens and starts leaving the sides of the pot.
5. Stir in 1 tsp pure vanilla extract and 1 tbsp avocado oil. Keep mixing until the mixture gathers into a soft dough and pulls cleanly from the bottom of the pot.
6. Transfer the hot dough to a lightly oiled surface. When it is cool enough to handle, oil your hands and knead it briefly until smooth and pliable.
7. Roll the dough between two sheets of parchment into a square about 1/4 inch thick. Let it cool for about 30 minutes, until set.
8. Cut into diamonds or small squares with a sharp knife or pizza cutter. Lift the pieces carefully and let them finish cooling before serving.

## Notes
- Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 1 week. Keep the pieces in a single layer or separate layers with parchment so they do not stick.
- If the dough feels dry or crumbly, warm it very briefly and knead again. If it feels sticky, use only a light dusting of cornstarch on the surface.
- For neater slices, let the slab cool fully before cutting. For a softer bite, roll it a little thicker and cut after it has set.
- This sweet firms up as it cools, so do not overcook the almond mixture once it starts pulling away from the pan.
