## Ingredients
- 1 tsp vegetable oil
- 1 onion, thinly sliced
- 12 garlic cloves, lightly crushed
- 3 x 5cm/2in pieces fresh root ginger, peeled and roughly chopped
- 8 red chillies, roughly chopped (use less if you prefer milder heat)
- 3 tbsp ground cumin
- 3 tbsp ground coriander
- 1 tbsp ground cinnamon
- 3 tbsp garam masala
- 3 tbsp tomato purée
- ½ small cauliflower head, cut into medium florets, root thickly sliced
- ½ small butternut squash, peeled and cut into 2cm/1in chunks
- 1 courgette, cut into 2cm/1in-thick slices
- 2 peppers, seeds removed, cut into large chunks
- 400g tin chopped tomatoes
- 1 reduced salt vegetable stock cube, crumbled
- 1.125 litres water, divided
- 300g/10½oz long-grain rice, such as basmati, rinsed
- 160g/5½oz frozen peas
- 1 lime, finely grated zest and juice
- salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 2 tbsp roughly chopped fresh coriander, to garnish

## Method
1. Blend 12 garlic cloves, 3 pieces of ginger, 8 red chillies, 3 tbsp ground cumin, 3 tbsp ground coriander, 1 tbsp ground cinnamon, 3 tbsp garam masala, 3 tbsp tomato purée and 125ml of the water until smooth. Use a quarter of the paste for this curry and freeze the rest for later.
2. Heat 1 tsp vegetable oil in a wok, saucepan or karahi over medium heat. Add 1 onion and cook for 3–4 minutes, stirring, until softened at the edges.
3. Stir in a quarter of the curry paste and cook for 2–3 minutes, until the spices smell toasted and the paste darkens slightly.
4. Add ½ small cauliflower head, ½ small butternut squash, 1 courgette and 2 peppers. Stir well so the vegetables are coated in the masala.
5. Pour in 400g chopped tomatoes, the remaining 1 litre water and 1 crumbled vegetable stock cube. Bring to a simmer and cook uncovered for 15–20 minutes, stirring now and then, until the vegetables are tender but still hold their shape.
6. Stir in 160g frozen peas for the last 2 minutes of cooking.
7. Add the zest and juice of 1 lime, then season with salt and black pepper to taste. Fold through 2 tbsp chopped coriander just before serving.
8. Meanwhile, cook 300g rinsed long-grain rice in a separate lidded pan with a little of the remaining water if needed. Keep it covered over low heat until the grains are tender and the rice is fluffy.

## Notes
- Keep the vegetables on the firmer side; they should hold their shape and not collapse into the sauce. If the pan looks dry before they are tender, add a splash of hot water.
- The remaining paste freezes well in small portions and is useful for another curry, or for starting a tray of roasted vegetables.
- Leftovers keep in the fridge for up to 3 days and reheat well with a splash of water to loosen the sauce. The flavour settles overnight, so the curry often tastes even better the next day.
- If you want a sharper finish, add a little extra lime juice at the table. Any mix of seasonal vegetables can work, as long as the firmer ones go in first and softer ones later.
