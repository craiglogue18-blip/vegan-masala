## Ingredients
- 2 cups plain flour
- 2 teaspoons ajwain seeds (carom seeds)
- 4 tablespoons cold vegan butter, cut into small cubes
- 1 tsp salt
- ½ to ¾ cup water, as needed
- 3 tbsp neutral oil
- 1 tablespoon ginger, minced
- 2 green chillies, chopped
- 1 teaspoon ground cumin
- 1 tablespoon ground coriander
- 2 teaspoons ground fennel, coarsely ground if possible
- 1 teaspoon amchur (dry mango powder, optional)
- ½ teaspoon turmeric
- 1½ pounds potatoes, boiled, peeled and cut into small pieces
- 1 cup green peas, thawed if frozen
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 750 ml neutral oil, for deep-frying

## Method
1. In a large bowl, combine 2 cups plain flour, 2 teaspoons ajwain seeds, 4 tablespoons cold vegan butter, and 1 tsp salt.
2. Rub the butter into the flour with your fingertips until the mixture looks like coarse crumbs.
3. Add ½ to ¾ cup water a little at a time and knead into a stiff, smooth dough.
4. Cover the dough and rest it for at least 30 minutes.
5. Heat 3 tbsp neutral oil in a pan over medium heat.
6. Add 1 tablespoon minced ginger and 2 chopped green chillies, and cook for about 30 seconds, just until the raw edge softens.
7. Stir in 1 teaspoon ground cumin, 1 tablespoon ground coriander, 2 teaspoons ground fennel, 1 teaspoon amchur if using, and ½ teaspoon turmeric.
8. Add 1½ pounds boiled potatoes and mix well so the spices coat the pieces.
9. Add 1 cup green peas and 1 teaspoon salt, then cook for 3 to 5 minutes until the filling is hot and the masala has settled into the potatoes.
10. Mash the filling lightly, leaving some potato pieces intact, then spread it out to cool completely.
11. Divide the dough into 7 equal portions and roll each portion into a ball.
12. Roll one ball into a thin circle about 4 to 5 inches across, then cut it in half.
13. Brush the straight edge with water and fold each half into a cone, pressing the seam firmly to seal.
14. Fill each cone with about 2 heaping teaspoons of the cooled filling, then pinch and fold the open edge closed.
15. Repeat with the remaining dough and filling.
16. Heat 750 ml neutral oil in a wok or deep pan until it reaches 165°C (325°F).
17. Fry the samosas in batches without crowding the pan, turning them as needed until evenly golden and crisp.
18. Lift out and drain on paper towels before serving hot.

## Notes
- The filling must be completely cool before shaping. Warm potato masala softens the dough and makes sealing harder.
- Cook the potato mixture until the spices taste rounded and the surface is no longer dusty. The filling should be dry, not wet.
- If the masala threatens to catch, lower the heat and add a spoonful of water before it darkens too much.
- For the crispest result, fry at a steady moderate temperature rather than rushing the heat. Too hot, and the pastry colours before the layers cook through.
- The filling can be made a day ahead and chilled. Cold filling is easier to portion and keeps the samosas neat.
- Leftover samosas reheat well in an oven or air fryer; avoid the microwave if you want the crust to stay crisp.
