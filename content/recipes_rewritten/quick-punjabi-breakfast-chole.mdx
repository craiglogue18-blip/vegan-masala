## Ingredients
- 1 can chickpeas (approx. 340g), drained and rinsed
- 1/2 tsp baking powder
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tea bag (orange pekoe or black tea is best)
- 3 cups water
- 2 tbsp oil
- 1 bay leaf
- 2 cardamom pods
- 1-inch cinnamon stick
- 1 tsp whole cumin seeds
- 1 onion, finely chopped
- 1 tsp ginger, grated
- 1 tsp garlic, minced
- 1 green chili, diced
- ½ tsp kasuri methi
- ¼ tsp turmeric powder
- 1 tsp chili powder
- 1 tsp coriander powder
- ½ tsp cumin powder
- ½ tsp garam masala
- 1 tsp aamchur
- 1½ cup crushed tomatoes (from a can or blended fresh tomatoes)
- ¼ tsp salt (or to taste)
- 3 tbsp oil
- 2 whole dried red chilies
- ¼ tsp turmeric powder
- ¼ tsp garam masala
- ¼ tsp red chili powder (optional)

## Method
1. In a saucepan, combine 1 can chickpeas, 1/2 tsp baking powder, 1 tsp salt, 1 tea bag, and 3 cups water. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat and simmer for 15 minutes, until the chickpeas are tender and lightly stained.
2. While the chickpeas cook, heat 2 tbsp oil in a wide pan over medium heat. Add 1 bay leaf, 2 cardamom pods, 1-inch cinnamon stick, and 1 tsp whole cumin seeds, and let them sizzle for 1 minute.
3. Add 1 finely chopped onion and cook for 10 to 15 minutes, stirring often, until deeply golden.
4. Stir in 1 tsp grated ginger, 1 tsp minced garlic, and 1 diced green chili. Cook for 1 to 2 minutes, until the raw smell fades.
5. Add ½ tsp kasuri methi, ¼ tsp turmeric powder, 1 tsp chili powder, 1 tsp coriander powder, ½ tsp cumin powder, ½ tsp garam masala, and 1 tsp aamchur. Stir for about 1 minute so the spices bloom in the onion base.
6. Add 1½ cup crushed tomatoes and cook over medium-low heat for 5 to 7 minutes, stirring regularly, until the tomatoes lose their raw edge and the masala looks thick.
7. Remove the tea bag from the chickpeas, then add the chickpeas with their cooking water to the pan. Stir in ¼ tsp salt or more to taste, bring to a gentle boil, and simmer for 5 to 10 minutes. Add a splash of water if you want a looser gravy.
8. For the tempering, heat 3 tbsp oil in a small pan. Add 2 whole dried red chilies, ¼ tsp turmeric powder, ¼ tsp garam masala, and ¼ tsp red chili powder if using. Cook for 1 to 2 minutes, just until fragrant.
9. Pour the hot tempering over the chole and serve right away.

## Notes
- The tea gives the chickpeas a darker colour and a deeper edge; black tea works better than a mild tea bag.
- If the gravy feels thin, simmer it a little longer before serving. If it thickens too much on standing, loosen it with a splash of hot water.
- Leftovers keep well in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat gently on the stovetop so the chickpeas stay intact and the masala does not catch at the bottom.
- Taste before serving and adjust salt or aamchur if the chickpeas need more lift.
- The tempering is optional, but it gives the finished chole a sharper aroma and a cleaner finish.
