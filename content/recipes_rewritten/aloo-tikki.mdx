## Ingredients
- 600g/1lb 5oz potatoes, peeled and cut into chunks
- 1½ tsp salt, divided
- 4 spring onions, white and green parts finely chopped
- 2–3 small chillies (bird’s eye or similar), finely chopped, seeds in
- handful fresh coriander, finely chopped
- 2 tsp ground coriander, divided
- 1 tsp cumin seeds, toasted and crushed
- 1 tsp ginger powder
- 3 tbsp panko breadcrumbs or any other breadcrumbs
- 2 tsp sunflower oil
- 4 tsp sunflower oil
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 2 tsp finely grated fresh root ginger
- 1 tsp red chilli powder
- ½ tsp ground turmeric
- remaining 1 tsp salt
- 5 tsp tomato purée
- 100ml water
- 300g/10½oz tinned chickpeas, drained and rinsed","handful fresh coriander, for garnish","tomato ketchup or tamarind chutney, to serve (optional)

## Method
1. Put the 600g potatoes in a saucepan, cover with water and add ½ tsp of the salt. Bring to a boil and cook until the potatoes are soft all the way through. Drain well and leave them in a large bowl until cool enough to handle.
2. Mash the cooled potatoes, then mix in 4 spring onions, 2–3 small chillies, a handful of fresh coriander, 1 tsp of the ground coriander, 1 tsp toasted and crushed cumin seeds, 1 tsp ginger powder and 3 tbsp breadcrumbs. Shape into 12 patties and press them lightly so they hold together.
3. Heat 4 tsp sunflower oil in a saucepan over medium heat. Add 1 tsp cumin seeds and let them sizzle, then add 2 tsp finely grated fresh root ginger. Cook for 1 minute, stirring often, then stir in 1 tsp red chilli powder, ½ tsp ground turmeric, the remaining 1 tsp salt, the remaining 1 tsp ground coriander and 5 tsp tomato purée.
4. Cook the masala briefly until the purée darkens a shade, then add 100ml water and 300g chickpeas. Simmer for 4–5 minutes, stirring now and then, until the sauce lightly coats the chickpeas.
5. Heat 2 tsp sunflower oil in a frying pan over medium heat. Fry the patties in batches for about 3 minutes on each side, until golden and crisp. Serve hot with the chickpea masala and a little fresh coriander on top.

## Notes
- Drain the potatoes well before mashing. Any extra moisture will make the tikki harder to shape and more likely to split in the pan.
- The mixture should feel firm and hold its shape when pressed. If it still feels soft, add a little more breadcrumbs, a teaspoon at a time.
- Cook the potato patties over medium heat so the crust colours without the centre going dry.
- The chickpea masala thickens as it sits. Add a splash of water when reheating if you want a looser sauce.
- Leftover tikki keep for up to 3 days in the fridge. Reheat in a dry frying pan or hot oven to bring back the crust.
- Make the chickpea masala ahead if you like, but fry the tikki close to serving so they stay crisp.
