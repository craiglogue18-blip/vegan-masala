## Ingredients
- 1 teaspoon oil
- 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds
- 1/2 cup chopped red or white onion
- 4 cloves garlic, minced (or 1 tablespoon garlic paste)
- 1 inch ginger, minced (or 1 tablespoon ginger paste)
- 1 teaspoon ground coriander
- 1 teaspoon garam masala
- 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika (or regular)
- 1/4 to 1/2 teaspoon cayenne (or red chili powder)
- 1.5 to 2 cups chopped vegetables (like cauliflower, green beans, broccoli)
- 1/4 teaspoon salt
- 1/4 cup water
- 1/2 cup thinly sliced or chopped carrot
- 1 cup finely chopped peppers (red, green, or other)
- 1/2 cup green peas (fresh or thawed if frozen)
- 3 cups cooked rice (preferably basmati; 1 cup dry rice will yield about 3 cups cooked)
- lemon juice, to taste
- remaining salt, as needed
- 1 cup plain, unsweetened non-dairy yogurt
- 1/4 cup water
- 1 teaspoon oil
- 1/2 teaspoon mustard seeds
- 6 curry leaves (optional)
- 1/4 cup thinly sliced red or white onion
- 1/2 teaspoon turmeric powder or ground turmeric

## Method
1. Heat 1 teaspoon oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds and let them crackle for about 1 minute.
2. Add 1/2 cup chopped onion, 4 cloves minced garlic, and 1 inch minced ginger. Cook for 3 minutes, stirring often, until the onion softens and the raw smell fades.
3. Stir in 1 teaspoon ground coriander, 1 teaspoon garam masala, 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika, and 1/4 to 1/2 teaspoon cayenne. Cook for 1 minute so the spices bloom in the oil.
4. Add 1.5 to 2 cups chopped vegetables, 1/4 teaspoon salt, and 1/4 cup water. Cook for 8 to 9 minutes, stirring now and then, until the firmer vegetables begin to soften.
5. Add 1/2 cup carrot, 1 cup peppers, and 1/2 cup green peas. Cover and cook for 3 to 4 minutes, until the vegetables are crisp-tender.
6. Add 3 cups cooked rice and lemon juice to taste. Toss well to break up any clumps, then cook for 2 minutes until the rice is hot through. Taste and add the remaining salt as needed.
7. While the rice finishes, whisk 1 cup plain unsweetened non-dairy yogurt with 1/4 cup water until smooth.
8. In a small saucepan, heat 1 teaspoon oil over medium heat. Add 1/2 teaspoon mustard seeds and cook until they pop, then add 6 curry leaves, if using, and 1/4 cup sliced onion. Cook for 3 minutes, stirring often, until the onion softens.
9. Stir in 1/2 teaspoon turmeric powder and the yogurt mixture. Simmer very gently for 2 minutes without boiling. Add a little more water if the raita feels too thick.
10. Serve the fried rice with the turmeric onion raita spooned over the top or on the side.

## Notes
- Cold leftover rice gives the best texture. If you cook rice fresh, spread it out and let it cool before adding it to the pan so the grains stay separate.
- Keep the vegetables varied. Firmer pieces build body, while quicker-cooking vegetables soften into the masala and help the rice feel cohesive.
- Do not boil the raita after the yogurt goes in; keep it at a gentle simmer. If it thickens on standing, loosen it with a spoonful of water.
- Store the rice and raita separately in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat the rice in a skillet with a splash of water; stir the raita well before reheating lightly.
- This dish takes lemon well, so finish with a little more if the rice tastes flat. The raita should be cool and lightly spiced, not sharp or sweet.
