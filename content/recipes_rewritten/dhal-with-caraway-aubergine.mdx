## Ingredients
- 1 large aubergine
- 4 tbsp olive oil, divided
- 1 tsp caraway seeds
- ½ tsp sea salt flakes, divided
- 1 onion
- 1 clove garlic
- ½ inch piece ginger
- 1 bay leaf
- ½ stick cinnamon
- ½ tsp cumin
- ½ tsp turmeric
- 170g red lentils
- 1 pint vegetable stock
- The juice of ½ a lemon
- roughly chopped coriander, to serve

## Method
1. Trim the aubergine ends and cut it into ½ inch chunks. Toss the aubergine with 3 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp caraway seeds and ½ tsp sea salt flakes, then leave it while you start the dhal.
2. Roughly chop 1 onion, then finely grate 1 clove garlic and ½ inch ginger. Heat the remaining 1 tbsp olive oil in a large saucepan over medium-low heat and cook the onion, garlic and ginger for about 20 minutes, stirring now and then, until soft and translucent.
3. Add 1 bay leaf, ½ stick cinnamon, ½ tsp cumin and ½ tsp turmeric. Fry for 3 minutes, stirring so the spices bloom without catching.
4. Stir in 170g red lentils until coated, then pour in 1 pint vegetable stock. Cover, bring to a boil, then lower to a gentle simmer for about 20 minutes. Stir only occasionally so the lentils keep some body.
5. While the dhal cooks, heat the oven to 180°C. Spread the aubergine on a baking tray in a single layer and roast for 30 to 35 minutes, turning once, until browned and tender.
6. When the lentils are soft and most of the liquid has been absorbed, remove the pan from the heat and beat the dhal with a wooden spoon until creamy but still textured.
7. Stir in the remaining ½ tsp sea salt flakes and the juice of ½ a lemon. Spoon into a shallow bowl, top with the roasted aubergine and finish with chopped coriander.

## Notes
- Beat the dhal well at the end if you want a smoother finish; leave it slightly looser than you want in the bowl, since it thickens as it stands.
- If the lentils catch or dry out before they soften, add a splash of hot water and keep the heat low.
- Leftovers keep well for up to 2 days in the fridge. Reheat gently on the hob with a little water to loosen the dhal, then top with fresh coriander if you have it.
- The aubergine should be deeply browned at the edges and soft in the middle; under-roasted pieces can taste heavy against the lentils.
