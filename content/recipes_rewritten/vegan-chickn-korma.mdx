## Ingredients
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 160 g plant-based chick'n (any brand)
- 1 white onion, finely chopped
- 4 garlic cloves, finely chopped
- A thumb-sized piece of ginger, finely chopped
- 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds
- 1/2 teaspoon coriander seeds
- 4 cardamom pods
- 1 tbsp garam masala
- 2 tsp ground turmeric
- 200ml rich coconut milk
- 200ml water
- 1 tbsp sugar
- 10g desiccated coconut
- A small handful of coriander, finely chopped
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper

## Method
1. In a pestle and mortar, crush 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds, 1/2 teaspoon coriander seeds and the seeds from 4 cardamom pods until coarsely broken.
2. Heat 2 tbsp neutral oil in a large frying pan over medium heat. Add 160 g plant-based chick'n and fry for 3 to 4 minutes, turning until lightly golden on several sides. Remove to a plate.
3. Add 1 finely chopped white onion to the same pan and cook for about 6 minutes, stirring often, until soft and lightly coloured. Stir in 4 finely chopped garlic cloves and a thumb-sized piece of finely chopped ginger, and cook for 1 minute.
4. Add 1 tbsp garam masala, 2 tsp ground turmeric and the crushed spices. Stir for 30 seconds, just until the spices darken slightly and smell cooked.
5. Return the chick'n to the pan. Pour in 200ml rich coconut milk, 200ml water and 1 tbsp sugar, then bring to a gentle simmer.
6. Cook over low heat for about 10 minutes, stirring now and then, until the sauce has reduced slightly and coats the chick'n in a smooth layer.
7. Stir in 10g desiccated coconut and a small handful of finely chopped coriander. Season with salt and freshly ground black pepper, then turn off the heat and leave the korma to sit for 10 minutes before serving.

## Notes
- The sauce should settle into a soft, spooning consistency. If it tightens too much, loosen it with a splash of hot water.
- Taste at the end and adjust the salt carefully; coconut milk needs enough seasoning to carry the spices.
- Leftovers keep well in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat gently over low heat so the coconut sauce does not split.
- This korma thickens a little as it stands, which suits rice well. If you want it looser for naan, add a little water while reheating.
