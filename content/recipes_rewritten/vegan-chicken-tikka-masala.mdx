## Ingredients
- 2 tablespoons neutral oil (sunflower, vegetable)
- 1 large onion, finely diced
- Salt
- 4 garlic cloves, grated
- A thumb-sized piece of ginger, grated
- 2 teaspoons garam masala
- 1 teaspoon chili powder (optional)
- 1 teaspoon cumin
- 1 teaspoon ground cardamom
- 1 teaspoon dried fenugreek leaves (methi, optional)
- 1 teaspoon turmeric
- 1 tablespoon tomato puree
- 1 x 400 g tin of finely chopped tomatoes or passata
- 1 x 400 g tin of coconut milk
- 320 g plant-based chick’n (any brand)
- Juice of 1/2 lemon
- A small handful of coriander, very finely chopped

## Method
1. Heat 2 tablespoons neutral oil in a large, heavy-based saucepan over medium-low heat. Add 1 large finely diced onion with a pinch of salt and cook for about 10 minutes, stirring now and then, until soft, translucent and starting to take on a little colour.
2. Add 4 grated garlic cloves and a thumb-sized piece of grated ginger, then cook for 1 minute until fragrant.
3. Stir in 2 teaspoons garam masala, 1 teaspoon chili powder if using, 1 teaspoon cumin, 1 teaspoon ground cardamom, 1 teaspoon dried fenugreek leaves if using, and 1 teaspoon turmeric. Add a splash of water and cook for 1 minute so the spices bloom without catching.
4. Mix in 1 tablespoon tomato puree and cook for 1 minute, then add 1 x 400 g tin of finely chopped tomatoes or passata, 1 x 400 g tin of coconut milk, and 320 g plant-based chick’n.
5. Simmer on medium-low heat for 20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens and the chick’n is heated through. Season with salt and the juice of 1/2 lemon.
6. Stir through a small handful of finely chopped coriander just before serving.

## Notes
- The sauce thickens as it stands. If it firms up on reheating, loosen it with a splash of water and warm it gently over low heat.
- Taste again at the end: coconut milk softens the spices, so the final salt and lemon matter.
- For a richer finish, use the full tin of coconut milk and simmer a few minutes longer so the masala coats the chick’n properly.
- Leftovers keep well for 2 to 3 days in the fridge and the spices settle in nicely overnight.
