## Ingredients
- 250g/9oz chana dal (split dried chickpeas), rinsed until the water runs clear
- 1 litre water, divided, plus more as needed
- 3 tbsp vegetable oil
- 1 tbsp cumin seeds
- 1 small onion, chopped
- 3–4 whole green chillies, pricked with a knife
- 2cm/¾in piece fresh root ginger, peeled and cut into thin strips
- 3 garlic cloves, peeled and left whole
- 3 medium tomatoes
- ¾ tsp ground turmeric
- ¾ tsp garam masala
- 1½ tsp ground coriander
- 1 tsp salt
- 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped

## Method
1. Put 250g/9oz chana dal in a saucepan with 900ml of the 1 litre water. Bring to a boil, skim off any foam, then cover and simmer for 35–40 minutes, stirring now and then, until the dal is soft and breaking down; add a little more water if the pan looks dry.
2. Remove the pan from the heat and whisk or mash the dal a little so it turns creamy but still keeps some texture. Set aside.
3. Heat 3 tbsp vegetable oil in a frying pan over medium heat. Add 1 tbsp cumin seeds and let them sizzle for 20–30 seconds.
4. Add 1 small chopped onion, 3–4 whole green chillies, and 2cm/¾in ginger cut into thin strips. Cook for 4–5 minutes until the onion softens and starts to turn golden at the edges.
5. Blend 3 garlic cloves and 3 medium tomatoes together until smooth. Add the mixture to the pan and stir well.
6. Add ¾ tsp ground turmeric, ¾ tsp garam masala, 1½ tsp ground coriander, the remaining 100ml water, and 1 tsp salt. Simmer for 15–20 minutes, stirring often, until the tomato has cooked down and the oil starts to separate at the sides.
7. Stir the cooked dal into the tarka. Add a little more water if needed, then bring it back to a gentle boil and check the seasoning.
8. Turn off the heat and stir through 2 tbsp chopped fresh coriander just before serving.

## Notes
- Chana dal needs steady simmering; if it is still firm after 40 minutes, keep cooking and add water a splash at a time so the pan does not catch.
- The dal thickens as it sits. Loosen it with hot water when reheating to bring back the right spooning texture.
- This keeps well in the fridge for up to 3 days and the flavour settles overnight. Reheat gently on the hob rather than boiling hard.
- If you want a sharper finish, add a little extra green chilli at the end rather than cooking it longer in the tarka.
