## Ingredients
- 1 tbsp cooking oil, divided
- 1 cup raw walnuts
- 1/3 cup black gram lentils (split and skinned)
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 7-8 green chilies or 2-3 Serrano peppers
- 1/2 inch ginger, peeled and cut
- 3/4 cup plain unsweetened vegan yogurt
- Salt to taste
- 1 tbsp frozen shredded coconut (optional)
- 1 tsp black mustard seeds
- 2-4 dried red chilies
- Few curry leaves (optional)
- 1/4 tsp cayenne pepper powder

## Method
1. Heat 1/2 tbsp cooking oil in a cast-iron skillet or pan over medium heat. Add 1 cup raw walnuts and roast until they smell nutty and turn lightly coloured.
2. Add 1/3 cup black gram lentils and 1 tsp cumin seeds. Stir until the lentils are lightly browned and the cumin smells toasted, then turn off the heat and let the mixture cool a little.
3. Transfer the walnut-lentil mixture to a blender. Add 7-8 green chilies or 2-3 Serrano peppers, 1/2 inch ginger, 3/4 cup plain unsweetened vegan yogurt, salt to taste, and 1 tbsp frozen shredded coconut if using.
4. Blend until smooth, adding a little water only if the blades need help moving. Taste and adjust salt or chilies if you want more heat.
5. Spoon the chutney into a serving bowl.
6. In the same pan, heat the remaining 1/2 tbsp cooking oil over medium heat. Add 1 tsp black mustard seeds and let them splutter.
7. Add 2-4 dried red chilies and a few curry leaves, if using. When the chilies darken, stir in 1/4 tsp cayenne pepper powder and remove from the heat.
8. Pour the tempering over the chutney and mix lightly before serving.

## Notes
- The chutney thickens in the fridge. Stir in a spoonful of water before serving if you want a looser spooning texture.
- It keeps well in a covered container in the fridge for up to 5 days. The flavour settles after a few hours and is often better the next day.
- Use fewer green chilies for a milder chutney. For sharper heat, keep some of the seed membranes in the chilies.
- The coconut is optional, but it rounds out the walnuts and softens the finish without changing the chutney’s character.
