## Ingredients
- 1 cup dried kidney beans, soaked overnight and rinsed
- 2 tbsp oil
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- a pinch of asafoetida (optional)
- 1 large onion, finely chopped
- 1 inch ginger, grated
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 green chillies, finely chopped
- 2 medium tomatoes, chopped
- 1/2 tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp chilli powder
- 2 tsp coriander powder
- 1 tsp garam masala
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 cup water
- 100 ml oat cream
- 1 tsp kasuri methi (optional)
- 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped
- a pinch of asafoetida

## Method
1. Cover 1 cup dried kidney beans with plenty of fresh water and soak them overnight. Drain and rinse before cooking.
2. Pressure cook the soaked beans until very soft and tender. Keep them aside with their cooking liquid.
3. Heat 2 tbsp oil in a pan and add 1 tsp cumin seeds. Let them sizzle, then add a pinch of asafoetida if using.
4. Add 1 large onion, finely chopped, and cook until deeply golden at the edges and the moisture has mostly gone.
5. Stir in 1 inch ginger, grated, 4 garlic cloves, minced, and 2 green chillies, finely chopped. Cook for a minute until the raw smell fades.
6. Add 2 medium tomatoes, chopped, and cook until they soften and break down into a thick masala.
7. Stir in 1/2 tsp turmeric, 1 tsp chilli powder, 2 tsp coriander powder, 1 tsp garam masala, and 1 tsp salt. Cook until the spices darken slightly and the masala looks glossy.
8. Add the cooked beans with 1 cup water. Simmer for 10 to 15 minutes, mashing a few beans against the pan to thicken the gravy.
9. Stir in 100 ml oat cream and 1 tsp kasuri methi if using. Simmer for 2 minutes, then finish with 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped.
10. Taste and adjust the seasoning before serving hot.

## Notes
- A slow onion cook and a well-reduced tomato base make the gravy taste finished, so do not rush that stage.
- If the rajma thickens too much, loosen it with a splash more bean cooking liquid or hot water.
- For a deeper body, mash more beans against the side of the pan before adding the cream.
- This reheats well. Keep it in the fridge for up to 3 days and warm it gently so the beans stay intact.
- The flavour settles after a few hours, so leftovers are often better the next day.
