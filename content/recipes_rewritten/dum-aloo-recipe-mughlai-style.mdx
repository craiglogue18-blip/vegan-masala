## Ingredients
- 3 medium potatoes
- 2 tbsp oil
- 1 tsp shahi jeera (caraway seeds)
- 1 small cinnamon stick
- 3 cloves
- 2 green cardamom pods
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 tsp fennel seeds
- 1 large onion, chopped
- 1 inch ginger, grated
- 3 garlic cloves
- 3 tbsp unsweetened plant yoghurt
- 1 tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp chilli powder
- 1 tsp garam masala
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 cup water
- 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped

## Method
1. Boil 3 medium potatoes until just tender. Drain, cool slightly, peel if needed, and prick them with a fork so the masala can move into the flesh.
2. Blend 1 tsp fennel seeds, 1 large chopped onion, 1 inch grated ginger, and 3 garlic cloves with a little water to a smooth paste. Stir in 3 tbsp unsweetened plant yoghurt until even.
3. Heat 2 tbsp oil in a heavy pan over medium heat. Add 1 tsp shahi jeera, 1 small cinnamon stick, 3 cloves, 2 green cardamom pods, and 1 bay leaf. Let the whole spices sizzle for a few seconds.
4. Add the onion paste and cook on low heat, stirring often, until the raw smell fades and the masala turns thick, darker, and glossy.
5. Add 1 tsp turmeric, 1 tsp chilli powder, 1 tsp garam masala, and 1 tsp salt. Cook for 1 minute, stirring so the spices bloom in the masala.
6. Add the potatoes and turn them gently so they are coated all over.
7. Pour in 1 cup water, bring to a gentle simmer, then cover and cook on low for 10 to 12 minutes.
8. Uncover and simmer for 1 to 2 minutes if the gravy needs thickening. Finish with 2 tbsp fresh coriander, then rest for a few minutes before serving.

## Notes
- Take time with the onion masala. It should lose its raw edge and cook down to a deep, glossy base; pale masala usually means a flat curry.
- The potatoes should be tender but not falling apart before they go into the pan. Pricking them helps the gravy reach the centre, not just the surface.
- Plant yoghurt adds body and a gentle tang. Stir it in evenly so the gravy stays smooth.
- This curry thickens as it sits. If reheating, add a splash of water and warm it slowly so the potatoes do not break up.
- For a looser gravy, keep back a little water and add it near the end rather than thinning the curry too much at the start.
- Leftovers keep well in the fridge for up to 3 days and often taste better the next day once the spices have settled.
