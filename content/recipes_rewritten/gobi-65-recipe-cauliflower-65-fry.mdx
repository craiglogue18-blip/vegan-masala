## Ingredients
- 1 small cauliflower, cut into florets
- 1 cup water
- 1/2 cup plain flour
- 1 cup rice flour
- 1/2 tsp black pepper
- 2 tsp chilli powder, divided
- 2 tsp garam masala, divided
- 1 tsp salt
- 500 ml neutral oil for deep-frying, plus 2 tbsp for the masala
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 green chillies, finely chopped
- a handful of curry leaves
- 3 tbsp unsweetened plant yoghurt

## Method
1. Blanch the cauliflower florets in 1 cup water for 2 to 3 minutes, then drain and rinse briefly. Leave them to dry well so the coating stays crisp.
2. In a mixing bowl, combine 1/2 cup plain flour, 1 cup rice flour, 1/2 tsp black pepper, 1 tsp chilli powder, 1 tsp garam masala, and 1 tsp salt. Add enough of the blanched cauliflower’s surface moisture only if needed, then mix to a thick batter that coats the back of a spoon.
3. Add the cauliflower florets to the batter and turn them until each piece is coated evenly.
4. Heat 500 ml neutral oil for deep-frying and fry the battered florets in batches until crisp and golden. Drain on a rack or paper towel.
5. Heat the remaining 2 tbsp oil in a pan. Add 1 tsp cumin seeds and let them crackle, then stir in 4 cloves garlic, 2 green chillies, and a handful of curry leaves.
6. Add the remaining 1 tsp chilli powder, remaining 1 tsp garam masala, and 3 tbsp unsweetened plant yoghurt. Cook for 1 to 2 minutes, stirring, until the yoghurt loses its raw edge and the masala thickens slightly.
7. Add the fried cauliflower and toss over high heat for 2 to 3 minutes, just until the masala clings to the florets without softening the crust.
8. Serve at once, while the coating is still crisp and the curry leaves are fresh on the nose.

## Notes
- Drying the cauliflower after blanching matters here; any extra water will loosen the batter and dull the crust.
- The plant yoghurt needs a short cook in the pan. If it goes in and out too quickly, it can taste raw and sit separate from the masala.
- Fry in batches and keep the oil hot, but not smoking, so the coating sets quickly instead of drinking in oil.
- If the batter thickens as it sits, loosen it with a spoonful of water. It should coat cleanly and not run off the florets.
- Leftovers will keep in the fridge for up to 3 days, though the crust softens. Reheat in an air fryer or hot oven to bring back some texture.
- Taste after the final toss and adjust salt or chilli then, since the masala should coat the cauliflower evenly before you judge the seasoning.
