## Ingredients
- 1 cup all-purpose flour (125 grams)
- 2 tablespoons gram flour (besan)
- ⅛ teaspoon turmeric powder
- 1 pinch baking soda (or ¼ teaspoon baking powder)
- 1 cup water
- 1 to 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour, as needed for thickening the batter
- 1 cup sugar (150 grams)
- ½ cup water
- ¼ teaspoon saffron strands
- ¼ teaspoon lime juice (optional, for the syrup)

## Method
1. In a mixing bowl, combine 1 cup all-purpose flour, 2 tablespoons gram flour, ⅛ teaspoon turmeric powder, and 1 pinch baking soda.
2. Add 1 cup water gradually and whisk until the batter is smooth and free of lumps. If needed, use a little more water to loosen it slightly, but keep it pourable and not thin.
3. Beat the batter with a spoon or whisk for about 4 minutes to aerate it, then cover and leave it in a warm place for 12 to 15 hours. In a cold kitchen, let it stand for 20 to 24 hours.
4. When the batter is fermented, it should look bubbly and a little lighter. Stir it gently, then add 1 to 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour only as needed to bring it back to a thick, flowing consistency.
5. Transfer the batter to a squeeze bottle or piping bag.
6. To make the syrup, combine 1 cup sugar, ½ cup water, and ¼ teaspoon saffron strands in a saucepan. Heat on low and stir until the sugar dissolves.
7. Simmer the syrup until it reaches one-thread consistency. Stir in ¼ teaspoon lime juice, if using, then keep the syrup warm over very low heat.
8. Heat oil in a kadai or deep pan to about 350°F (176°C). Test with a drop of batter; it should rise at once without sinking.
9. Pipe the batter into the hot oil in small concentric spirals. Keep the shapes compact so they fry evenly.
10. Fry until the jalebi is golden and crisp, turning once if needed. Lift it out with a skewer or tongs and let the oil drip back into the pan.
11. Immerse the hot jalebi in the warm syrup for 2 to 3 minutes, turning once so both sides are coated.
12. Lift out and drain on a tray lined with parchment or foil. Fry and soak the remaining batter the same way.
13. Serve warm, or let it cool to room temperature if you want a firmer bite.

## Notes
- Jalebi is best on the day it is made, when the shell is crisp and the syrup is still fresh inside. After a few hours, it softens as it sits.
- If the batter is too thin, the spirals spread in the oil; if it is too thick, they are hard to pipe. Adjust with a small amount of flour or water before frying.
- Keep the syrup warm, not boiling, or it will make the jalebi sticky on the outside and heavy in the middle.
- Refrigerated leftovers will lose some crispness. Reheat briefly in a low oven, then serve at once.
- Fry in batches and avoid crowding the pan, so the oil temperature stays steady and the jalebi colour stays even.
