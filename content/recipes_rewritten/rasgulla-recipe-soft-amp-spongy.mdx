## Ingredients
- 1 litre whole milk
- 2 to 3 tablespoons lemon juice, divided
- 1 teaspoon sooji (rava or semolina) or all-purpose flour or cornstarch
- 2 cups sugar
- 1 litre water
- 1 tablespoon plant-based milk, optional
- 1 to 2 tablespoons rose water (or kewra water or ½ teaspoon cardamom powder)

## Method
1. Bring 1 litre of whole milk to a boil in a heavy pan over low to medium heat, stirring now and then so it does not catch at the bottom.
2. Keep a strainer lined with cheesecloth or muslin ready while the milk heats.
3. Lower the heat to the minimum and add 1 tablespoon of lemon juice, stirring gently until the milk curdles. Add the remaining lemon juice only if needed to separate the curds fully.
4. When the curds have separated and the whey looks clear and greenish, turn off the heat.
5. Pour the curds into the lined strainer and rinse well under cool running water to wash away the sourness.
6. Gently squeeze out excess water, then let the chenna drain under a weight for 7 to 8 minutes, or hang it for about 30 minutes.
7. The chenna should feel soft and moist, not wet or crumbly. Add 1 teaspoon sooji and knead for about 10 minutes until smooth and slightly greasy.
8. Divide the chenna into small portions and roll them into smooth balls. Cover them with a damp kitchen towel so they do not dry out.
9. In a large pot, combine 2 cups sugar and 1 litre water. Stir in 1 tablespoon plant-based milk, if using, and bring the syrup to a boil.
10. If any scum rises, skim it off or strain the syrup through cheesecloth.
11. Keep aside 1 cup of the syrup for later. Bring the remaining syrup back to a steady boil.
12. Slide the chenna balls into the boiling syrup carefully. Do not stir; just nudge the pot very gently if needed.
13. Cover and cook over medium heat for 4 minutes. Add ¼ cup of the reserved syrup, cover again, and cook for 4 minutes more.
14. Add another ¼ cup of the reserved syrup, cover, and cook for 2 more minutes.
15. To check, press one rasgulla lightly; it should spring back. It should also sink if dropped into a bowl of water.
16. Move the rasgullas into the reserved syrup. Let the syrup cool slightly, then add 1 to 2 tablespoons rose water and mix very gently.
17. Leave the rasgullas to soak for at least 30 minutes before serving.

## Notes
- Moisture matters most with chenna. If it is too wet, the rasgullas may split; if it is too dry, they turn dense and crack when cooked.
- Knead only until the dough feels smooth and supple. Over-kneading makes the balls heavy.
- Use a wide pot so the rasgullas have space to puff without crowding each other.
- Store the rasgullas in their syrup in the refrigerator for up to 1 week. They are best served cold or at cool room temperature.
- If the syrup thickens after chilling, loosen it with a splash of water before serving.
- Rose water, kewra water, or cardamom powder can be used for the final aroma; use one, not all three.
