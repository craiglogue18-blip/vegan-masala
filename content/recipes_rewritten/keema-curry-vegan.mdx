## Ingredients
- 150g dried soya mince (or use 400g fresh or frozen plant-based mince)
- 250ml water
- 3 tbsp oil, divided
- 2 large onions, finely diced
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tsp sugar
- 4 large cloves garlic, crushed
- 1 tbsp grated ginger
- 2 dried bay leaves (optional)
- 2 tsp smoked paprika (hot or mild)
- 2 tsp ground cumin
- 2 tsp ground coriander
- 1/2 tsp ground turmeric
- 1 x 400g can chopped tomatoes or passata
- 1 tbsp dark soy sauce (or tamari for a gluten-free option)
- 50ml oat cream (or unsweetened plant milk for a lighter option)
- 1 1/2 tsp cornflour (cornstarch)
- 1 tbsp garam masala
- 225g frozen peas
- 2 tbsp chopped coriander leaves
- 1 tbsp chopped mint leaves
- Chopped green chillies, mint and fried onions (garnish, optional)
- Plant-based butter (to serve, optional)

## Method
1. Put 150g dried soya mince in a bowl, cover with hot water, and leave for 10 minutes. Drain well in a fine sieve, rinse under cold water, and squeeze out the excess moisture. If you are using 400g fresh or frozen plant-based mince, skip this step.
2. Heat 2 tbsp of the oil in a large, heavy-based pan over medium heat. Add 2 large finely diced onions, 1 tsp salt, and 1 tsp sugar. Cook for about 10 minutes, stirring now and then, until the onions are deep golden and soft.
3. Stir in 4 crushed garlic cloves, 1 tbsp grated ginger, and 2 dried bay leaves, if using. Cook for 1 to 2 minutes, until the raw smell fades.
4. Add the drained soya mince and cook over high heat for 5 to 6 minutes, stirring often, until it picks up some colour and a few edges turn crisp. Lift it out and set aside.
5. In a small bowl, whisk 50ml oat cream with 1 1/2 tsp cornflour until smooth and lump-free.
6. Return the pan to medium-low heat and add the remaining 1 tbsp oil. Stir in 2 tsp smoked paprika, 2 tsp ground cumin, 2 tsp ground coriander, and 1/2 tsp ground turmeric. Bloom the spices for about 15 seconds, just until fragrant.
7. Add 1 x 400g can chopped tomatoes or passata and 1 tbsp dark soy sauce. Cook for 1 to 2 minutes, scraping up any browned bits from the base of the pan.
8. Stir in the browned soya mince, the oat cream and cornflour mixture, 250ml water, and 1 tbsp garam masala. Mix well, then bring to a gentle simmer.
9. Cover and cook for 20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the masala has thickened and the tomato base tastes cooked through. For a drier keema, uncover for the last few minutes and let the excess moisture cook off.
10. Stir in 225g frozen peas, 2 tbsp chopped coriander leaves, and 1 tbsp chopped mint leaves. Cook for the final 5 minutes, just until the peas are hot and the herbs have settled into the curry.
11. Taste and adjust the salt if needed. Serve hot with the optional green chillies, mint, fried onions, and a little plant-based butter, if using.

## Notes
- This curry thickens as it stands. When reheating, add a small splash of water and warm it gently so the mince stays tender.
- If you use fresh or frozen plant-based mince, give it enough time in the pan to brown at the edges before the tomatoes go in. That step gives the curry better texture.
- For a softer, saucier keema, stop the simmer a little earlier. For a drier version, cook uncovered for the last few minutes and stir often.
- Leftovers keep well in the fridge for 3 days and freeze for up to 1 month. Defrost fully before reheating so the texture stays even.
