## Ingredients
- 2 tbsp oil
- 1/4 tsp fenugreek seeds
- 1 tsp mustard seeds
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 1 large onion, finely chopped
- 3 medium potatoes, cubed
- 1 inch ginger, grated
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped
- 1 tsp chilli powder
- 1/2 tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp salt
- 2 medium tomatoes, chopped
- chopped fresh methi (fenugreek) leaves

## Method
1. Heat 2 tbsp oil in a wide pan over medium heat. Add 1/4 tsp fenugreek seeds, 1 tsp mustard seeds and 1 tsp cumin seeds, and let them crackle.
2. Add 1 large onion, finely chopped, and cook until soft and lightly golden at the edges.
3. Stir in 3 medium potatoes, cubed, and cook for 1–2 minutes so they pick up the oil and seeds.
4. Add 1 inch ginger, grated, and 4 cloves garlic, minced. Cook for about a minute, just until the raw smell fades.
5. Mix in 1 tsp ground cumin, 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped, 1 tsp chilli powder, 1/2 tsp turmeric and 1 tsp salt.
6. Add 2 medium tomatoes, chopped, and cook until they break down and the masala turns thick and glossy.
7. Add chopped fresh methi (fenugreek) leaves and stir well so they wilt through the potatoes.
8. Cover the pan and cook on low heat, stirring now and then, until the potatoes are tender. Add a small splash of water only if the pan looks dry.
9. Uncover and cook for a few minutes more until the curry is semi-dry and the potatoes are coated in the masala.

## Notes
- Use a wide pan so the potatoes cook evenly and the masala can reduce without sticking.
- Fresh methi softens quickly; add it near the end so it keeps its colour and stays pleasantly earthy.
- If the pan starts to catch, lower the heat and add a small splash of water. The onions should turn golden, not dark.
- For a softer curry, cover a little longer; for a drier finish, cook uncovered at the end until the masala clings to the potatoes.
- Leftovers keep well in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat gently in a pan so the potato pieces hold their shape.
- The flavour settles after resting, so it is often even better later in the day or the next day.
