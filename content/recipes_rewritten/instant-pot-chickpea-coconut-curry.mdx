## Ingredients
- 2 tbsp oil
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 large onion, finely chopped
- 1 inch ginger, grated
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 red chilli, finely chopped
- 1 tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp garam masala
- 200 ml tomato puree
- 1 tin chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tin coconut milk
- 200 g spinach
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped

## Method
1. Set the Instant Pot to sauté and heat 2 tbsp oil.
2. Add 1 tsp cumin seeds and 1 bay leaf. Let them sizzle for a few seconds.
3. Stir in 1 large onion, finely chopped, and cook until soft and turning golden at the edges.
4. Add 1 inch ginger, grated, 4 cloves garlic, minced, and 1 red chilli, finely chopped. Cook for 1 minute, stirring so the garlic does not catch.
5. Mix in 1 tsp turmeric and 1 tsp garam masala, then add 200 ml tomato puree. Cook until the masala thickens and the raw tomato smell cooks off.
6. Add 1 tin chickpeas, drained and rinsed, 1 tsp salt, and 1 tin coconut milk. Stir well and scrape up anything from the base.
7. Seal the Instant Pot and pressure cook on high for 3 minutes.
8. Leave for 5 minutes on natural release, then release the remaining pressure.
9. Stir in 200 g spinach and let it wilt into the curry.
10. Finish with 1 tbsp lemon juice and 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped. If you want a thicker sauce, simmer on sauté for a few minutes with the lid off.
11. Serve hot.

## Notes
- Cook the onion base properly before pressure cooking; that gives the curry its depth and keeps the tomato from tasting sharp.
- Coconut milk softens the spice and rounds the sauce, so taste again after cooking and adjust salt or lemon at the end.
- Spinach should go in after pressure cooking. If it goes in too early, it loses colour and turns too soft.
- The sauce thickens a little as it stands. For a looser curry, stop after the spinach goes in; for a fuller finish, reduce it briefly on sauté.
- Leftovers keep well for 3 days in the fridge. Reheat gently so the coconut milk stays smooth and the chickpeas do not break down.
