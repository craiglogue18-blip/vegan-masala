## Ingredients
- 2 tbsp oil
- 2 red onions, sliced
- 3 tbsp rogan josh curry paste
- 1 red chilli, finely chopped
- 1 inch ginger, grated
- 2 medium tomatoes, chopped
- 3 medium sweet potatoes, cubed
- 1 tin chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 1 tin coconut milk
- 200 g spinach
- 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped

## Method
1. Heat 2 tbsp oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add 2 sliced red onions and cook until soft and lightly golden.
2. Stir in 3 tbsp rogan josh curry paste and 1 finely chopped red chilli. Cook for 1 minute, stirring so the spices bloom in the oil.
3. Add 1 inch grated ginger and 2 medium chopped tomatoes. Cook until the tomatoes break down and the masala looks thicker and darker at the edges.
4. Add 3 medium cubed sweet potatoes and 1 tin chickpeas, drained and rinsed. Stir well so they are coated in the masala.
5. Pour in enough water to just cover the vegetables. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat, cover, and simmer until the sweet potatoes are tender.
6. Remove the lid and cook for a few minutes more until the sauce reduces and lightly clings to the vegetables.
7. Stir in 1 tin coconut milk and simmer gently for 3 to 4 minutes without letting it boil hard.
8. Add 200 g spinach and cook just until wilted. Finish with 2 tbsp chopped fresh coriander and serve.

## Notes
- Cook the onions properly at the start; they give the curry its body and keep the sauce from tasting flat.
- If the masala starts sticking, lower the heat and add a splash of water. Letting it catch will give the curry a bitter edge.
- Sweet potatoes should be tender but still hold their shape. If they are cut large, the curry will need a little longer.
- The curry thickens as it stands, so loosen it with a splash of water when reheating.
- Leftovers keep well in the fridge for up to 3 days and freeze cleanly for a practical batch-cook meal.
