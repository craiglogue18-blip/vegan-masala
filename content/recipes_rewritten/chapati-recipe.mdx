## Ingredients
- 2 cups whole wheat flour (atta, sifted)
- 1 teaspoon kosher salt
- 3 tablespoons oil, divided
- 1 cup water, plus more if needed
- ½ cup whole wheat flour, for dusting

## Method
1. In a large bowl, mix 2 cups whole wheat flour, 1 teaspoon kosher salt and 1 tablespoon of the oil until the flour is lightly coated.
2. Pour in 1 cup water a little at a time and knead until the dough is soft and smooth. Work in 1 teaspoon more oil, then cover and rest the dough for 20 to 30 minutes. Keep the remaining oil for shaping and cooking.
3. Divide the dough into lemon-sized pieces. Lightly dust one piece with some of the ½ cup whole wheat flour for dusting and roll it into a 4-inch circle.
4. Spread a few drops of the reserved oil on the circle, sprinkle a little flour over it, fold it in half, then repeat the oil and flour once more before folding into a triangle.
5. Roll the triangle into a chapati about 8 to 9 inches wide. If the dough starts to stick, use a little more of the dusting flour.
6. Heat a cast-iron tawa or heavy skillet over medium-high heat until very hot. Place one chapati on the pan and cook for 30 to 40 seconds, until small bubbles form.
7. Flip the chapati and cook the second side. Press it gently with a kitchen towel or spatula so it puffs without tearing.
8. Flip once more, then brush or dab both sides with a thin film of the remaining oil. Cook until golden spots appear, then lift it off the pan.
9. While the chapati is still hot, clap it gently between your hands or in a clean kitchen towel to separate the layers.
10. Keep the chapati covered as you finish the rest, and serve warm.

## Notes
- Soft dough is the main thing here. If it feels tight while kneading, add water a teaspoon at a time until it relaxes.
- A properly hot tawa helps the chapati puff and keeps the surface from drying out before the centre cooks.
- Use enough heat for light brown spotting, not a fast burn. If the pan runs too hot, lower it for the next round.
- For best texture, cook chapatis close to serving time and keep them wrapped in a clean towel or covered container so they stay soft.
- Leftover chapatis keep well for 2 to 3 days in the fridge in an airtight container. Reheat on a hot tawa; a few seconds over direct flame will soften them further if needed.
