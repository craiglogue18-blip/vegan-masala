## Ingredients
- 1 ½ cups water
- 1 ½ cups sugar
- 8 green cardamom pods, crushed to release the seeds
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 2 ½ cups white bread crumbs
- ½ cup cashews, blended with ½ cup water into a very smooth cream
- Vegetable oil, for deep frying
- 2 tbsp chopped nuts, such as cashews, almonds, or pistachios, for garnish (optional)

## Method
1. In a saucepan, combine 1 ½ cups water and 1 ½ cups sugar. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat and add 8 crushed green cardamom pods and 1 tbsp lemon juice. Simmer for 5 minutes, then turn off the heat and keep the syrup warm.
2. Put 2 ½ cups white bread crumbs in a mixing bowl. Add ½ cup cashew cream a little at a time, mixing until the crumbs form a smooth, pliable dough that holds together without feeling wet or sticky.
3. Divide the dough into 14 portions and roll each one into a smooth ball. Press away any cracks with lightly oiled hands so the surface stays even.
4. Heat vegetable oil in a deep pan to about 150°C / 300°F. The oil should be steady and gently bubbling, not smoking hot.
5. Fry the balls a few at a time, turning them gently so they colour evenly. Cook until they are deep golden to reddish brown and cooked through.
6. Lift them out and drain briefly on paper towels. While they are still warm, prick each one a few times with a thin skewer or pin so the syrup can seep in.
7. Transfer the warm jamuns into the warm syrup and make sure they are fully submerged. Leave them to soak for at least 3 hours before serving.
8. Serve with a little syrup spooned over the top and scatter 2 tbsp chopped nuts, if using.

## Notes
- Keep the syrup warm, not boiling, when the fried balls go in. Hot syrup can toughen the outside before it has time to soak through.
- If the dough feels dry, add a teaspoon of cashew cream at a time; if it turns soft or greasy, add a few more bread crumbs and shape it again.
- The jamuns keep well in their syrup for 2 to 3 days at room temperature in cool weather, or in the fridge for longer. Warm them gently before serving if you want them softer.
- For the best texture, let them soak fully before serving. They firm up a little as they cool, then soften again as the syrup settles in.
