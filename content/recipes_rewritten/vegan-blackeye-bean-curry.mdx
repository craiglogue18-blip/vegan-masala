## Ingredients
- 2 x 410g/14oz tins black-eyed beans, drained and rinsed (or 225g/8oz dried beans, soaked overnight and cooked)
- 1 tbsp cumin seeds
- 1 tbsp coriander seeds
- 1 tsp fennel seeds
- ¼ tsp fenugreek seeds
- 1cm/½in stick cinnamon
- ½ - 2 tsp crushed chilli flakes
- 4 tbsp vegetable oil
- 1 tsp black mustard seeds
- 10 curry leaves (fresh or dried)
- 1 medium onion, finely chopped
- 2 garlic cloves, crushed
- 5cm/2in piece fresh ginger, grated
- salt to taste
- 550ml/1 pint water
- 2 tbsp coriander leaves, chopped
- 2 tbsp fresh coconut, grated (optional, but do not use desiccated)

## Method
1. If using tinned beans, drain and rinse the 2 x 410g/14oz tins of black-eyed beans. Lightly mash a few beans with a fork so the curry has some body.
2. Set a small heavy-based frying pan over medium heat. Add 1 tbsp cumin seeds, 1 tbsp coriander seeds, 1 tsp fennel seeds, ¼ tsp fenugreek seeds and 1cm/½in stick cinnamon. Toast for a few minutes, stirring often, until fragrant and the coriander seeds deepen in colour.
3. Tip the toasted spices into a grinder or mortar. Grind to a fine powder, then stir in ½ - 2 tsp crushed chilli flakes and set aside.
4. Heat 4 tbsp vegetable oil in a medium pot over medium heat. Add 1 tsp black mustard seeds and 10 curry leaves, then cover straight away as they crackle.
5. Return the pot to the heat and add 1 medium onion, finely chopped. Cook, stirring often, until soft, translucent and golden at the edges.
6. Add 2 garlic cloves, crushed, and 5cm/2in piece fresh ginger, grated. Cook for about 30 seconds, just until the raw aroma fades.
7. Stir in the beans, the ground spice mixture, salt to taste and 550ml/1 pint water. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat.
8. Cover and simmer for 20 minutes, stirring now and then, until the beans are seasoned through and the gravy has a slight thickness.
9. Stir through 2 tbsp coriander leaves, chopped, and 2 tbsp fresh coconut, grated, if using. Taste and adjust the salt before serving.

## Notes
- For a thicker gravy, uncover the pot for the last 5 minutes so a little water cooks off. A few lightly mashed beans help the sauce turn creamy without losing the bean texture.
- If you are using dried beans, cook them fully before they go into the curry. Undercooked beans stay firm in the middle and do not take on the masala properly.
- This keeps well in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat gently with a splash of water, since the beans continue to thicken the sauce as they sit.
- Fresh coconut softens the spice at the end, but the curry stands up well without it. Dried curry leaves can work, though fresh leaves give a cleaner aroma.
