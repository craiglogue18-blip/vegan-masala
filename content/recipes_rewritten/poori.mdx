## Ingredients
- 2 cups whole wheat flour, plus extra for dusting
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 cup water, added gradually as needed for the dough; some oil for frying

## Method
1. In a mixing bowl, combine 2 cups whole wheat flour and 1/2 teaspoon salt.
2. Add 1 tablespoon oil and rub it into the flour with your fingertips.
3. Pour in 1 cup water a little at a time, kneading until you have a firm, smooth dough.
4. Cover the dough with a damp cloth and rest it for 20 to 30 minutes.
5. Divide the dough into small balls, about the size of a golf ball.
6. Dust a work surface lightly with extra flour and roll each ball into a thin disc about 5 inches wide.
7. Heat enough oil for frying in a deep pan over medium heat.
8. When the oil is hot, slide in one disc at a time.
9. Fry each poori for about 30 seconds per side, turning once, until it puffs and turns golden.
10. Lift out with a slotted spoon and drain on paper towels. Serve at once.

## Notes
- Keep the dough firm; soft dough makes poori absorb too much oil and lose its puff.
- Roll the discs evenly, without dusting with too much flour, so they cook quickly and rise in the oil.
- If the oil is not hot enough, the poori will go pale and heavy; if it is too hot, it will brown before puffing.
- Poori is best eaten right after frying, while the surface is crisp and the centre is still soft.
- Leftovers can be kept in the fridge for up to 3 days, then warmed in a low oven for a few minutes; they will not stay puffed.
- Serve with dal, aloo sabzi, chana masala, or a simple pickle and chutney.
