## Ingredients
- 12 ounces (340-345g) Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled and washed
- 16 ounces (454g) high-protein or super-firm tofu, crumbled
- 5 tablespoons cornstarch (or arrowroot powder or tapioca flour)
- 1 tablespoon minced or grated fresh ginger
- 1 teaspoon garam masala
- 1 teaspoon Indian red chili powder
- Kosher salt or sea salt to taste
- Vegetable oil for frying
- 1 cup raw cashews, soaked in boiling water for 15 minutes and drained
- 4-6 whole green cardamom pods
- 2 cinnamon sticks, broken into small pieces
- 4 whole cloves
- 1 teaspoon cumin seeds
- 1 tablespoon unrefined coconut oil (or a neutral oil of choice)
- 1 extra large yellow onion, chopped
- 1-2 green chilies, deseeded and chopped
- 1 ½ tablespoons finely chopped fresh ginger
- 4 garlic cloves, roughly chopped
- ½ teaspoon ground coriander
- 1-2 teaspoons Indian red chili powder (adjust for heat)
- 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
- 3 extra large tomatoes, chopped with juices
- 1 ¼ cups (300 mL) unsweetened plant-based milk (or water)
- 1 tablespoon lemon juice
- 1 tablespoon dried fenugreek leaves (optional)
- ½ teaspoon garam masala
- 1 cup loosely packed fresh cilantro, chopped (stems included)
- Indian bread (naan or paratha) or white rice

## Method
1. Put the 12 ounces (340-345g) Yukon Gold potatoes in a saucepan, cover with cold water, and salt well. Bring to a boil and cook until just fork-tender, 10 to 12 minutes, then drain and leave to dry.
2. Add the drained 1 cup raw cashews, 3 extra large chopped tomatoes with juices, 1 ¼ cups (300 mL) unsweetened plant-based milk, and 1 tablespoon lemon juice to a blender. Blend until smooth and set aside.
3. Heat 1 tablespoon unrefined coconut oil in a heavy-bottomed pan over medium-high heat. Add 4-6 green cardamom pods, 2 cinnamon sticks, 4 cloves, and 1 teaspoon cumin seeds, and cook until fragrant, about 45 to 60 seconds.
4. Add 1 extra large yellow onion and cook until softened and lightly golden, 4 to 5 minutes. Stir in 4 garlic cloves, 1 ½ tablespoons fresh ginger, and 1-2 green chilies and cook for 1 minute more.
5. Add ½ teaspoon ground coriander, 1-2 teaspoons Indian red chili powder, and 1 teaspoon ground turmeric. Stir for 30 seconds, then add the blended tomato-cashew mixture and bring it to a simmer.
6. Season with 1 ½ teaspoons kosher salt, cover, and simmer for 15 minutes, stirring now and then, until the gravy thickens and the raw tomato smell cooks off.
7. Blend the gravy again until completely smooth, then return it to the pan. Crush in 1 tablespoon dried fenugreek leaves, if using, and stir in ½ teaspoon garam masala and 1 cup chopped cilantro. Keep warm on low.
8. Pulse the dried potatoes in a food processor until crumbly, then add 16 ounces (454g) crumbled tofu and pulse just until the mixture looks like a coarse, soft ricotta.
9. Add 5 tablespoons cornstarch, 1 tablespoon minced or grated fresh ginger, 1 teaspoon garam masala, 1 teaspoon Indian red chili powder, and salt to taste. Mix until the dough holds together, then shape into 18 kofta, about 40 to 50 grams each.
10. To fry, heat about 2 inches of vegetable oil in a wok over medium heat. Fry a small bit of mixture first; it should sizzle and float without browning too fast. Fry the kofta in batches for 5 to 8 minutes, turning until crisp and golden. For baking, heat the oven to 425ºF (218ºC) and bake on a lined tray for 30 to 35 minutes, turning halfway.
11. Warm the gravy gently, then add the kofta just before serving and spoon sauce over the top. Serve straight away with naan, paratha, or rice, and keep any extra kofta and gravy separate.

## Notes
- The kofta soften as they sit in gravy, so add them at the last minute if you want a crisp edge to hold. If you are making the dish ahead, keep the gravy and kofta in separate containers.
- The gravy reheats well over low heat; add a splash of water if it has thickened in the fridge. Kofta can be refreshed in a hot oven or dry skillet before serving.
- Taste the gravy before serving and adjust salt or chili to suit your table. The cashew base should be smooth and balanced, not heavy.
- If the kofta mixture feels too soft to shape, chill it for 15 to 20 minutes before rolling. If it seems dry, add a spoonful of plant milk or a little water.
- For a lighter version, bake the kofta rather than frying them. They will be a little less crisp, but the texture still holds well in the gravy.
