## Ingredients
- 3 to 4 medium sweet potatoes, cut into bite-sized cubes (no need to peel)
- 2 tablespoons olive oil or aquafaba, divided
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- fresh cracked pepper, to taste
- 1 vidalia or yellow onion, chopped
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tablespoon fresh ginger, minced (about a thumb-sized piece)
- 1 teaspoon curry powder
- 2 teaspoons cumin
- 2 teaspoons coriander
- 1 1/2 teaspoons turmeric
- 1 1/2 teaspoons garam masala
- 1 1/2 teaspoons salt
- 15 oz can chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 15 oz can unseasoned diced tomatoes
- 1 cup vegetable broth
- 15 oz can reduced-fat coconut milk
- 1/4 teaspoon nutmeg
- 1 tablespoon lemon juice
- fresh cilantro, for garnish

## Method
1. Heat the oven to 450°F (230°C).
2. Toss the sweet potatoes with 1 tablespoon of the olive oil or aquafaba, 1/2 teaspoon salt, and fresh cracked pepper. Spread them on a lined baking sheet in a single layer.
3. Roast for about 30 minutes, turning once if needed, until the edges are browned and the pieces are tender through.
4. While the sweet potatoes roast, warm the remaining 1 tablespoon olive oil or aquafaba in a skillet over medium heat. Add the chopped onion and cook for 3 to 5 minutes, stirring often, until softened and translucent.
5. Add the garlic, ginger, curry powder, cumin, coriander, turmeric, and garam masala. Stir for 1 minute, just until the spices smell toasted and the pan looks coated in masala.
6. Stir in the chickpeas, diced tomatoes, vegetable broth, 1 1/2 teaspoons salt, and nutmeg. Cook for 2 minutes, scraping up anything stuck to the pan.
7. Pour in the coconut milk and lemon juice. Lower the heat and simmer gently for 3 to 5 minutes, until the sauce thickens slightly.
8. Add the roasted sweet potatoes and fold them through the curry. Simmer for 5 minutes more so the potatoes take on the sauce, then finish with cilantro and serve hot.

## Notes
- If you want a looser curry, add a little more vegetable broth at the end. For a thicker finish, simmer uncovered for a few extra minutes after adding the sweet potatoes.
- The salt level may need a small adjustment at the end, especially if your tomatoes or broth are already seasoned.
- Leftovers keep well in the refrigerator for up to 3 days. Reheat gently on the stovetop with a splash of water or broth so the sauce loosens again.
- This curry holds its shape well for serving with rice or flatbread, and the sweet potatoes soften further overnight without turning mushy.
