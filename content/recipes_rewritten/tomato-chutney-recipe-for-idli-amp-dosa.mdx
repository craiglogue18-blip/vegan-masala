## Ingredients
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 teaspoon urad dal (split and husked black gram)
- 2 to 3 dry red chilies, broken and deseeded
- 1 to 2 cloves (optional)
- 4 to 5 black peppercorns
- 1 inch ginger, peeled and chopped
- 1.5 cups tomatoes, chopped (about 225 to 250 grams or 2 large tomatoes)
- 1 pinch asafoetida (hing; optional)
- salt, to taste
- 2 tablespoons water, for blending or grinding; adjust as needed
- ½ tablespoon oil (sesame oil, sunflower oil, or any neutral oil)
- ½ teaspoon mustard seeds
- 7 to 8 curry leaves
- 2 to 3 fenugreek seeds (methi seeds)
- 1 pinch asafoetida (hing)
- 1 dry red chili, halved and deseeded

## Method
1. Heat 1 tablespoon oil in a frying pan over low heat. Add 1 teaspoon urad dal and cook until it turns a deep maroon-brown without scorching.
2. Add 2 to 3 dry red chilies, 1 to 2 cloves if using, 4 to 5 black peppercorns, and 1 inch ginger. Stir for a few seconds until the chilies darken slightly and the ginger loses its raw smell.
3. Add 1.5 cups tomatoes, 1 pinch asafoetida, and salt to taste. Cook on medium-low heat for 6 to 7 minutes, stirring often, until the tomatoes soften and reduce.
4. If the mixture starts sticking before the tomatoes break down, add a small splash of water and keep cooking until it looks thick and jammy.
5. Turn off the heat and let the mixture cool slightly.
6. Transfer to a small blender or chutney grinder. Add 2 tablespoons water, or a little more if needed, and grind until smooth.
7. Heat ½ tablespoon oil in the same pan over medium heat. Add ½ teaspoon mustard seeds and let them crackle.
8. Add 7 to 8 curry leaves, 2 to 3 fenugreek seeds, 1 pinch asafoetida, and 1 dry red chili. Let the curry leaves crisp for a few seconds, but keep the spices from browning too far.
9. Add the blended chutney back to the pan and cook on low heat for 3 to 4 minutes. Add a little hot water if it feels too thick.
10. Taste and adjust salt if needed. Serve warm or at room temperature.

## Notes
- Cook the urad dal slowly. A deep brown gives the chutney a nuttier base; if it darkens too fast, lower the heat right away.
- The tomatoes need to reduce properly. If they stay wet, the chutney will turn loose and taste thin.
- Keep the tempering brief. Fenugreek seeds and curry leaves burn quickly, and even a little burnt methi will make the chutney bitter.
- This chutney thickens as it cools. Stir in a spoonful of warm water when reheating if needed.
- It keeps well in the fridge for 1 to 2 days. Reheat gently and taste again for salt after chilling.
- For a sharper chutney, use all the dried chilies; for a milder one, seed them well before cooking.
