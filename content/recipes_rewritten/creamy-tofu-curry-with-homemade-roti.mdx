## Ingredients
- 400g firm tofu
- 2 tbsp cornflour
- 4 tbsp vegetable oil, divided
- 5 tbsp tikka or madras paste
- 2 white onions, thinly sliced
- 1 tbsp tomato purée
- 500g tomato passata
- 200g creamed coconut
- 15g coriander, roughly chopped (optional)
- 100g plain or wholemeal flour plus extra for dusting
- 1 tsp vegetable oil for the roti

## Method
1. Heat the oven to 230C/210C fan/gas 8. Drain 400g firm tofu on kitchen paper, press it dry, then tear it into bite-sized pieces. Toss with 2 tbsp cornflour, 1 tbsp of the vegetable oil, and a pinch of salt. Spread on a baking tray and bake for 25 minutes, turning once if needed, until the edges are crisp and golden.
2. While the tofu bakes, make the roti dough. Mix 100g plain or wholemeal flour, 1 tsp vegetable oil, a pinch of salt and about 65ml water to form a soft dough. Knead briefly until smooth, then cover and leave to rest.
3. Set a frying pan over medium heat with the remaining 3 tbsp vegetable oil. Add 5 tbsp tikka or madras paste and cook for 1 minute, stirring, until the spices darken slightly and smell cooked through.
4. Add 2 white onions, thinly sliced, and cook for 4 to 5 minutes, stirring often. If the paste starts to catch, add a splash of water. Cover and cook for 8 to 10 minutes more, until the onions are fully soft and glossy.
5. Stir in 1 tbsp tomato purée and cook for 1 minute. Add 500g tomato passata and a pinch of salt, then simmer for 5 minutes so the sauce thickens and the raw tomato taste cooks out.
6. Stir in 200g creamed coconut and let it melt into the sauce over low heat. If the curry becomes too thick, loosen it with a little water.
7. Divide the rested roti dough into 4 pieces. Roll each one on a lightly floured surface to 2 to 3mm thick, then cook in a very hot dry pan for about 1 minute on each side, until lightly blistered and charred in spots. Keep them wrapped in a tea towel as they finish.
8. Add the baked tofu to the curry and turn it through the sauce until well coated. Serve hot, finished with 15g chopped coriander if using.

## Notes
- For firmer tofu, press it for 15 to 20 minutes before baking. The coating should feel dry before it goes into the oven; that is what gives the edges some bite.
- The curry thickens as it sits because of the coconut. Keep a little water nearby and loosen it when reheating so the sauce stays smooth.
- Store the curry and roti separately in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat the curry gently in a pan and warm the roti in a dry pan or wrapped in foil.
- If you want more greens in the meal, stir in spinach at the end and cook just until wilted.
- Taste and adjust salt before serving, especially if your tikka or madras paste is well seasoned already.
