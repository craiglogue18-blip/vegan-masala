## Ingredients
- 400 g onions, finely sliced
- 100 g gram flour (besan)
- 30 g rice flour
- 1 tsp baking powder
- 1/2 tsp turmeric
- 1/2 tsp cayenne pepper
- 1/2 tsp ground cumin
- 1/2 tsp ground coriander
- 1/2 tsp sea salt
- 150 ml water
- 750 ml neutral oil, for deep-frying

## Method
1. Put the 400 g finely sliced onions in a large mixing bowl.
2. Add 100 g gram flour, 30 g rice flour, 1 tsp baking powder, 1/2 tsp turmeric, 1/2 tsp cayenne pepper, 1/2 tsp ground cumin, 1/2 tsp ground coriander, and 1/2 tsp sea salt.
3. Toss everything together so the spices and flours cling to the onions before adding any water.
4. Pour in 150 ml water a little at a time, mixing well, until the onions are coated in a thick, rough batter that holds together without looking wet.
5. Heat 750 ml neutral oil in a deep pan over medium-high heat until a small piece of batter sizzles and rises at once.
6. Drop spoonfuls of the onion mixture into the oil, working in batches so the pan stays uncrowded.
7. Fry until the underside is deep golden, then turn and cook the other side until crisp and evenly browned.
8. Lift the bhajis out with a slotted spoon and drain on kitchen paper.
9. Serve immediately while the edges are still crisp.

## Notes
- Slice the onions evenly so they soften at the same rate in the hot oil.
- Add the water gradually; too much will make the batter slack and the bhajis heavy.
- Keep the oil hot between batches. If it cools too much, the bhajis will take on oil instead of crisping cleanly.
- These are at their best straight from the pan. To reheat, use a hot oven or air fryer rather than the microwave.
- Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days.
