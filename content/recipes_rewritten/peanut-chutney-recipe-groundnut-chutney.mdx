## Ingredients
- 1 tablespoon oil (any neutral oil)
- 2 tablespoons chana dal (20 grams, husked and split Bengal gram)
- ½ cup peanuts (80 grams, raw)
- 12 to 15 curry leaves (small to medium-sized)
- 2 to 3 green chilies (or dry red chilies)
- 2 garlic cloves (small to medium-sized)
- 1 pinch asafoetida (hing, optional)
- 1 tablespoon white sesame seeds (10 grams)
- salt (to taste)
- ¾ cup water (or as needed)

## Method
1. Heat 1 tablespoon oil in a pan over low heat and add 2 tablespoons chana dal.
2. Stir often and roast until the chana dal turns deep golden. Remove it from the pan right away so it does not darken further.
3. Add ½ cup peanuts to the same pan and roast over low to medium-low heat for 4 to 5 minutes, until they are crisp and lightly browned.
4. Add 12 to 15 curry leaves, 2 to 3 green chilies, 2 garlic cloves, and 1 pinch asafoetida. Sauté for about 1 minute, until the curry leaves crisp and the garlic loses its raw smell.
5. Stir in 1 tablespoon white sesame seeds and turn off the heat.
6. Let the mixture cool to room temperature, then transfer it to a blender with the roasted chana dal and salt to taste.
7. Add ¾ cup water and blend to a smooth chutney. Add a little more water only if the chutney needs loosening.
8. Transfer to a bowl and serve.
9. Taste and adjust salt if needed before serving.

## Notes
- For a thicker chutney, keep the water on the lower side. Add extra water only after blending if the chutney feels too tight.
- The chutney thickens as it sits, especially after refrigeration. Stir in a spoonful of water to bring it back to a pouring consistency.
- Store in an airtight container in the refrigerator for 2 to 3 days. Give it a good stir before serving, since the peanut oil may separate slightly.
- Dry red chilies give a sharper, deeper heat than green chilies. Use them if you want a more traditional, slightly darker chutney.
- This chutney suits idli, dosa, vada, and uttapam, and it also works as a spread for sandwiches or wraps.
