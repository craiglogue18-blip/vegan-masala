## Ingredients
- 1.5 cups basmati rice (300 grams, rinsed and soaked for 20 to 30 minutes)
- 1 teaspoon cumin seeds (or caraway seeds)
- 5 to 6 whole black peppercorns, optional
- 1 tej patta (Indian bay leaf)
- 4 cloves
- 3 to 4 green cardamoms
- 1 black cardamom, optional
- 1 small piece of mace, optional
- 1 small star anise, optional
- 1 inch cinnamon stick
- 1 small piece of stone flower (dagad phool or patthar ke phool), optional
- 3 tablespoons vegan ghee or neutral oil
- 1 cup thinly sliced onions (or 1 large onion, sliced)
- 1 to 2 green chillies, finely chopped or crushed to a paste
- 1 to 1.5 inches ginger, crushed to a paste
- 4 to 5 garlic cloves, crushed to a paste
- ½ cup chopped tomatoes (or 1 medium tomato, chopped)
- ½ to ¾ cup cauliflower, chopped
- ½ to ¾ cup potatoes, chopped
- ¼ cup carrots, chopped
- ⅓ cup green peas, fresh or frozen
- ¼ cup green beans, chopped
- ¼ cup green bell pepper, chopped, optional
- ¼ cup baby corn, sliced or chopped, optional
- 2.5 to 3 cups water or vegetable stock
- salt, as required
- 3 tablespoons coriander leaves, chopped
- 2 tablespoons mint leaves, chopped, optional
- ¼ teaspoon lemon juice, optional
- water, as needed for soaking

## Method
1. Rinse 1.5 cups basmati rice until the water runs mostly clear, then soak it in enough water for 20 to 30 minutes. Drain well before cooking.
2. Crush 1 to 2 green chillies, 1 to 1.5 inches ginger and 4 to 5 garlic cloves to a coarse paste, or use a mortar and pestle for a little texture.
3. Heat 3 tablespoons vegan ghee or neutral oil in a deep, heavy-bottomed pot over medium heat. Add 1 teaspoon cumin seeds, 5 to 6 black peppercorns, 1 tej patta, 4 cloves, 3 to 4 green cardamoms, 1 black cardamom, 1 small piece of mace, 1 small star anise, 1 inch cinnamon stick and 1 small piece of stone flower, if using. Let the spices sizzle for a few seconds.
4. Add 1 cup thinly sliced onions and cook on medium-low heat until they are deep golden at the edges and the raw smell has gone.
5. Stir in the ginger-garlic-chilli paste and cook for a few seconds, just until the mixture stops smelling sharp.
6. Add ½ cup chopped tomatoes and cook for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring, until they soften and begin to break down into the masala.
7. Add ½ to ¾ cup cauliflower, ½ to ¾ cup potatoes, ¼ cup carrots, ⅓ cup green peas, ¼ cup green beans, ¼ cup green bell pepper, if using, and ¼ cup baby corn, if using. Sauté for 1 to 2 minutes so the vegetables take on the masala.
8. Fold in the drained rice and stir gently for 1 to 2 minutes, coating the grains without breaking them.
9. Pour in 2.5 to 3 cups water or vegetable stock and add salt to taste. Stir once, then bring the pot to a steady boil.
10. Lower the heat, cover tightly and cook until the rice is tender and the liquid is absorbed. If the rice needs a little more time, add a splash of hot water and keep the texture loose rather than wet.
11. Turn off the heat and leave the pulao covered for 5 minutes. Fluff with a fork, then finish with 3 tablespoons coriander leaves, 2 tablespoons mint leaves, if using, and ¼ teaspoon lemon juice, if using.

## Notes
- A heavy pot helps keep the rice from sticking, and soaking the basmati gives you longer, cleaner grains. Once the water goes in, keep the heat low and avoid stirring.
- If you use vegetable stock, taste it before adding salt. A well-seasoned stock can carry the dish on its own, so you may need less salt than with plain water.
- Cut the vegetables small and keep the heat controlled so they cook through without turning soft. The potatoes should be tender, not crumbly, by the time the rice is done.
- Leftovers keep well for up to 3 days in the fridge. Reheat with a splash of water, covered, so the rice loosens without drying out.
- This pulao holds its shape well for lunchboxes, but it is best fluffed only once after resting. Too much handling will break the grains.
- The optional whole spices add a deeper aroma, but the pulao still works well with the basic spice set. Keep the onions and tomatoes in place; they give the dish its body and balance.
