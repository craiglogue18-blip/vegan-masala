## Ingredients
- 1 tsp oil
- 2 teaspoons cumin seeds
- 2 bay leaves
- 4 green cardamom pods
- 4 cloves
- 2 cups raw basmati rice
- 4 cups water, at room temperature
- Salt to taste
- A generous pinch of saffron

## Method
1. Heat 1 tsp oil in a skillet over medium heat. Add 2 teaspoons cumin seeds, 2 bay leaves, 4 green cardamom pods and 4 cloves. Let the spices sizzle until the cumin darkens slightly and smells nutty.
2. Stir in 2 cups raw basmati rice and cook for about 1 minute, until the grains look opaque at the edges and feel lightly coated in the spice oil.
3. Pour in 4 cups water and add salt to taste; it should taste a little saltier than you want the rice to be. Add a generous pinch of saffron and bring to a boil over medium-high heat.
4. When the water starts to drop below the surface of the rice, give it a gentle stir once or twice so nothing catches on the bottom. Cover the pan tightly, lower the heat to the smallest flame and cook for 15 minutes.
5. Turn off the heat and leave the rice covered for 10 minutes. Fluff gently with a fork so the grains stay long and separate.

## Notes
- A little toasting in the spiced oil helps the rice keep its shape, but do not let it take on colour.
- The rice should finish tender with a clean bite and no wet layer at the bottom. If it still feels firm after resting, cover it again over very low heat for a few more minutes.
- For leftovers, cool the rice quickly and keep it in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat with a splash of water in a covered pan, or steam it gently until hot.
- Brown rice can be used, but it needs more water and a longer cook, so do not follow the basmati timing here.
- Keep the salt steady and the spices restrained. Jeera rice should read as cumin rice, not a spiced pilaf.
