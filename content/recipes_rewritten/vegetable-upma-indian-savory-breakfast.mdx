## Ingredients
- 1 tablespoon vegan ghee or neutral oil
- 1/4 teaspoon black mustard seeds
- 1/4 teaspoon cumin seeds
- 1 teaspoon split urad dal (optional)
- 1 teaspoon chana dal (optional)
- 1/2 cup onions, finely chopped
- 1 teaspoon ginger, finely chopped
- 1/2 teaspoon green chili or jalapeño, finely chopped
- 4-6 curry leaves (optional)
- 2 cups mixed vegetables (carrots, zucchini, green beans, frozen green peas, etc.)
- 1 3/4 cups water
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- 1 cup roasted wheat semolina
- 1/4 cup cilantro, chopped
- 1/2 lime or lemon, freshly squeezed

## Method
1. Set a medium saucepan over medium heat and add 1 tablespoon vegan ghee or neutral oil. Add 1/4 teaspoon black mustard seeds and 1/4 teaspoon cumin seeds. If using, add 1 teaspoon split urad dal and 1 teaspoon chana dal, then stir until the seeds pop and the dals turn light golden.
2. Add 1/2 cup finely chopped onions, 1 teaspoon finely chopped ginger, 1/2 teaspoon finely chopped green chili or jalapeño, and the 4-6 curry leaves if using. Cook for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring often, until the onions soften and begin to take on colour.
3. Stir in 2 cups mixed vegetables and cook for a minute. Add a few tablespoons of water, cover, and cook for 5 to 10 minutes, until the vegetables start to soften but still hold their shape.
4. Pour in 1 3/4 cups water and add 1/2 teaspoon salt. Bring it to a boil, then lower the heat and slowly sprinkle in 1 cup roasted wheat semolina while stirring constantly to keep the mixture smooth.
5. Cover and cook on low for 5 minutes, until the water is absorbed and the semolina is tender. Turn off the heat and leave it covered for 2 minutes.
6. Fluff with a spoon, then mix in 1/4 cup chopped cilantro and the juice from 1/2 lime or lemon. Taste and adjust with a little more salt or citrus if needed.

## Notes
- Roast the semolina in advance if you are not using store-bought roasted sooji; it keeps the upma light and stops it from tasting raw or pasty.
- The vegetables can be changed to suit what you have, but keep the pieces small so they cook at the same pace as the semolina.
- If the upma sits too long and tightens, loosen it with a splash of hot water and stir gently over low heat.
- Leftovers keep well in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat with a little water in a pan rather than the microwave if you want to bring back the softer texture.
- A little extra lime at the table wakes it up nicely, especially if you are serving it with chutney or pickle.
