## Ingredients
- ½ cup raw basmati rice
- 6 cups oat milk
- ½ cup sugar (or substitute with date syrup, maple syrup, agave nectar, stevia, or erythritol)
- ½ tsp ground cardamom
- A generous pinch of saffron
- 2 tablespoons raisins
- 2 tablespoons sliced almonds (optional, or use pumpkin seeds instead)
- ½ tsp vanilla extract

## Method
1. Soak ½ cup raw basmati rice in enough water to cover it by 2 to 3 inches for 1 hour. Drain well and leave the rice un-rinsed so the starch stays on the grains.
2. Put 6 cups oat milk and the drained rice in a large saucepan. Bring it to a gentle simmer over low to medium-low heat, then cook for 15 to 20 minutes, stirring now and then, until the rice is very soft and the milk has started to thicken.
3. Stir in ½ cup sugar and keep cooking for about 15 minutes, stirring often so the kheer does not catch on the bottom. The mixture should reduce slightly and look creamier.
4. Add ½ tsp ground cardamom, a generous pinch of saffron, 2 tablespoons raisins, and 2 tablespoons sliced almonds. Cook for a few minutes more, until the raisins plump and the pudding looks glossy and well combined.
5. Take the pan off the heat and stir in ½ tsp vanilla extract. Cover the kheer and let it cool to room temperature so it settles into a soft pudding.
6. Serve chilled or slightly cool.

## Notes
- Kheer thickens as it cools. If it sets too firmly, loosen it with a splash of oat milk and warm it gently while stirring.
- The rice should be fully tender and broken down a little, with the milk still soft and creamy rather than tight or pasty.
- Leftovers keep well in the refrigerator for up to 3 days. Stir before serving, since the rice settles and the surface can thicken.
- If you are using a liquid sweetener instead of sugar, add it a little at a time and taste as you go, since the sweetness and body will change.
- For extra texture, finish each bowl with more sliced almonds or pumpkin seeds just before serving.
