## Ingredients
- 3 medium potatoes, peeled and thinly sliced
- 1 cup chickpea flour (besan)
- 1 teaspoon cumin seeds
- 1 teaspoon turmeric powder
- 1 teaspoon red chilli powder
- 1 teaspoon garam masala
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 cup water
- 500 ml neutral oil, for deep-frying

## Method
1. Peel 3 medium potatoes and slice them thinly so they cook through while the batter crisps.
2. In a large bowl, combine 1 cup chickpea flour (besan), 1 teaspoon cumin seeds, 1 teaspoon turmeric powder, 1 teaspoon red chilli powder, 1 teaspoon garam masala, and 1 tsp salt.
3. Pour in 1 cup water gradually, stirring until the batter is thick and smooth enough to coat the back of a spoon.
4. Add the sliced potatoes to the batter and turn gently until every slice is well covered.
5. Heat 500 ml neutral oil in a deep, heavy pan to 175°C, or until a small drop of batter rises at once and turns golden in steady bubbles.
6. Fry the battered potatoes in batches, without crowding the pan, until the coating is crisp and evenly golden on both sides.
7. Lift out with a slotted spoon and drain on kitchen paper before serving.
8. If needed, keep the fried pakoras warm in a low oven while you finish the rest of the batch.

## Notes
- Slice the potatoes evenly and fairly thin; thick slices take too long to cook and the coating can overcolour before the centre softens.
- The batter should cling to the potatoes rather than run off. If it loosens while standing, add a little more besan before frying.
- Keep the oil at a steady medium heat. Too hot and the outside colours too fast; too cool and the pakoras absorb oil.
- These are at their best straight from the pan, when the crust is crisp and the potato inside is soft.
- Leftovers keep in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat in a hot oven or air fryer until the coating firms up again; avoid the microwave if you want to keep the texture.
