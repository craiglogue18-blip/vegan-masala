## Ingredients
- 2 cups rice (white basmati rice recommended)
- ½ cup urad dal (black gram dal)
- 2 tablespoons chana dal (Bengal gram dal)
- ½ teaspoon fenugreek seeds (methi seeds)
- ½ cup poha (flattened rice)
- 1½ cups water, divided
- Salt to taste
- Vegetable oil or cooking spray for greasing the griddle

## Method
1. In a large bowl, combine 2 cups rice, ½ cup urad dal, 2 tablespoons chana dal, ½ teaspoon fenugreek seeds, and ½ cup poha. Cover with enough water to submerge the grains by 2 to 3 inches and soak for 8 hours.
2. Drain well and transfer the soaked mixture to a blender. Add 1 cup of the 1½ cups water and blend to a smooth batter with a faint grain from the rice.
3. Check the batter. It should be thick and pourable; add the remaining ½ cup water a little at a time only if needed to loosen it.
4. Transfer the batter to a large bowl, cover, and leave it in a warm place for at least 8 hours or overnight, until it looks airy and lightly risen.
5. Stir in salt to taste after fermentation. The batter should fall from the spoon in a steady ribbon; if it feels too tight, add a small splash of water.
6. Heat a griddle over medium-high heat until a few drops of water sizzle and disappear within 5 seconds.
7. Pour a ladleful of batter into the centre of the griddle and spread it quickly in a thin spiral with the base of the ladle.
8. Drizzle a little vegetable oil around the edges, or use cooking spray if you prefer. If using spray, lower the heat briefly before spraying.
9. Cook until the edges turn lacy and golden and the surface looks dry. Ease the dosa off with a flat spatula and serve at once.
10. Repeat with the remaining batter, stirring it lightly between dosas if it settles.

## Notes
- Fermentation depends on room temperature. In a warm kitchen, the batter can be ready sooner; in a cool kitchen, give it more time until it smells gently sour and looks risen.
- If the batter thickens after fermenting, loosen it with water a tablespoon at a time. Keep it on the pourable side; a batter that is too thick will not spread thinly.
- A properly heated griddle matters here. If the pan is underheated, the dosa can stick and the edges will stay pale instead of turning crisp.
- Leftover batter keeps well in the fridge for up to 2 days. Stir it gently before cooking and let it sit out for a little while so it spreads evenly.
- Cooked dosas are best served right away. If you need to reheat one, use a hot griddle rather than a microwave to keep the texture intact.
