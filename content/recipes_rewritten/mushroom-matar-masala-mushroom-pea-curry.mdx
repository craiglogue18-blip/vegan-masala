## Ingredients
- 3/4 cup red onion, chopped
- 5 cloves garlic
- 1 inch ginger, peeled
- 1 hot green chili, such as Serrano or Indian, seeds removed for less heat if desired
- 1 tsp oil
- 2 large tomatoes, chopped (or 14 oz can of diced tomatoes)
- 1/4 cup raw cashews (soaked for 15 minutes if needed; 1/3 cup for a creamier sauce)
- 1 to 2 tsp garam masala
- 1 tsp paprika
- 1 tsp dried fenugreek leaves (kasuri methi)
- 6 to 8 oz sliced white mushrooms (or cremini or baby bella, thinly sliced)
- 15 oz can of chickpeas, drained and rinsed (or 1 1/2 cups cooked chickpeas)
- 1/2 cup water
- 1/2 tsp salt, or to taste
- 1/4 tsp sugar, or to taste
- Cayenne pepper, to taste
- 3/4 cup peas, fresh or frozen
- 1 cup chopped spinach (optional)

## Method
1. Blend 3/4 cup red onion, 5 cloves garlic, 1 inch peeled ginger, and 1 hot green chili with a few tablespoons of water to a smooth paste.
2. Heat 1 tsp oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the onion paste and cook for 5 to 7 minutes, stirring often, until the raw smell is gone and the paste looks reduced.
3. Blend 2 large chopped tomatoes with 1/4 cup raw cashews until smooth, adding a little water only if the blender needs help.
4. Add the tomato-cashew purée to the skillet with 1 to 2 tsp garam masala, 1 tsp paprika, and 1 tsp dried fenugreek leaves. Cook for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring, until the masala darkens slightly and the oil starts to separate at the edges.
5. Stir in 6 to 8 oz sliced mushrooms, 15 oz drained chickpeas, 1/2 cup water, 1/2 tsp salt, and 1/4 tsp sugar. Mix well and bring to a simmer.
6. Cover and cook for 10 minutes, checking once or twice so the masala does not catch at the bottom. Add a little more water if it becomes too thick.
7. Taste and adjust the salt, then add cayenne pepper to taste if you want more heat. Stir in 3/4 cup peas and 1 cup chopped spinach, if using, and cook for 1 to 2 minutes until the peas are hot and the spinach has wilted.
8. Finish with fresh cilantro and serve hot.

## Notes
- The finished curry should be thick and spoonable, with the mushrooms still holding their shape. If the sauce tightens on standing, loosen it with a splash of water while reheating.
- This keeps well in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat gently over low heat so the cashews stay smooth and the mushrooms do not turn rubbery.
- If you want a richer gravy, use the larger amount of cashews. For a lighter sauce, use the smaller amount and keep the simmer short once the tomatoes have cooked down.
