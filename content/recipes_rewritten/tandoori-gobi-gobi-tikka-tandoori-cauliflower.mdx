## Ingredients
- 1 small to medium cauliflower, cut into medium florets
- black salt, for the boiling water and to taste
- ½ to ¾ cup plant-based yogurt
- ½ tablespoon ginger garlic paste
- ½ or 1 teaspoon red chili powder (or cayenne pepper)
- ½ or 1 teaspoon garam masala (or tandoori masala)
- ½ teaspoon turmeric powder
- 1 teaspoon coriander powder
- 1 teaspoon chaat masala
- ½ teaspoon carom seeds (ajwain)
- 1 teaspoon dried fenugreek leaves (kasoori methi)
- 2 tablespoons roasted gram flour (besan)
- 1 or 2 teaspoons lemon juice
- 1 tablespoon oil

## Method
1. Bring a pot of water to a boil with a small pinch of black salt. Add the cauliflower florets and simmer for 3 to 4 minutes, just until they begin to soften.
2. Turn off the heat and leave the cauliflower in the hot water for 7 to 8 minutes, then drain very well and let the florets steam-dry for a few minutes.
3. If the plant-based yogurt is loose, drain it in a fine sieve set over a bowl for 45 minutes to 1 hour before mixing.
4. In a mixing bowl, combine ½ to ¾ cup plant-based yogurt, ½ tablespoon ginger garlic paste, ½ or 1 teaspoon red chili powder, ½ or 1 teaspoon garam masala, ½ teaspoon turmeric powder, 1 teaspoon coriander powder, 1 teaspoon chaat masala, ½ teaspoon carom seeds, 1 teaspoon dried fenugreek leaves, 2 tablespoons roasted gram flour, 1 or 2 teaspoons lemon juice, 1 tablespoon oil, and black salt to taste.
5. Add the drained cauliflower and coat each floret gently, taking care not to break the florets. Taste the marinade and adjust the salt or heat if needed.
6. Cover and refrigerate for at least 1 hour. If you have more time, let it sit longer so the spices settle into the cauliflower.
7. Heat the oven to 200°C (395°F) for 15 minutes. Line a tray with foil or parchment and set a rack over it.
8. Arrange the marinated cauliflower in a single layer on the rack.
9. Roast or grill for 20 to 25 minutes, turning halfway through, until the edges are browned and the stem feels tender.
10. Transfer to a platter and serve hot.
11. Serve with mint chutney, sliced onion, and lemon wedges.

## Notes
- Use yogurt that is thick enough to cling to the florets. If yours is watery after draining, stir in a little extra besan rather than thinning it with more liquid.
- Parboiling helps the cauliflower cook through in the oven, but keep it brief. If it softens too much at this stage, the florets can fall apart later.
- For milder gobi tikka, use the lower amount of red chili powder. For a sharper finish, use the higher amount and keep the lemon juice bright.
- Leftovers keep well in the fridge for 2 days. Reheat in an oven or air fryer so the coating firms up again; the microwave will soften the crust.
- If you want more browning, finish under the broiler for 1 to 2 minutes, watching closely so the masala does not catch and turn bitter.
