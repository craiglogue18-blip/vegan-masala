## Ingredients
- 2 tbsp oil
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- a pinch of asafoetida (optional)
- 1 large onion, finely chopped
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 inch ginger, grated
- 2 green chillies, finely chopped
- 2 medium tomatoes, chopped
- 1 tsp chilli powder
- 2 tsp coriander powder
- 1/2 tsp turmeric powder
- 200g dried black chickpeas, soaked overnight, drained and rinsed
- 1 cup water
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tsp garam masala
- 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped

## Method
1. Soak 200g dried black chickpeas overnight. Drain them well and rinse under running water.
2. Heat 2 tbsp oil in a pressure cooker or heavy pot over medium heat. Add 1 tsp cumin seeds and let them crackle.
3. Add a pinch of asafoetida, if using, then add 1 large onion, finely chopped. Cook slowly, stirring often, until it turns light golden at the edges.
4. Stir in 4 cloves garlic, minced, 1 inch ginger, grated, and 2 green chillies, finely chopped. Cook for 1 minute until the raw smell settles.
5. Add 2 medium tomatoes, chopped, and cook until they break down and the masala looks thick and glossy.
6. Add 1 tsp chilli powder, 2 tsp coriander powder, and 1/2 tsp turmeric powder. Stir for 30 seconds so the spices bloom in the hot masala.
7. Add the soaked chickpeas, 1 cup water, and 1 tsp salt. Stir well, then cover and pressure cook for 10 to 12 whistles, or simmer covered in a pot until the chickpeas are fully tender.
8. When the chickpeas are cooked, stir in 1 tsp garam masala and simmer for 2 to 3 minutes. Mash a few chickpeas against the side of the pot if you want the gravy thicker.
9. Finish with 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped, and serve hot.

## Notes
- Take time with the onion and tomato base. If the masala still tastes sharp, the curry will stay thin and uneven even after the chickpeas are cooked.
- Kala chana should hold its shape but yield cleanly when pressed. If you are simmering in a pot, add a little more water as needed so the chickpeas cook through without catching.
- For a thicker gravy, mash a small handful of chickpeas into the masala at the end instead of boiling the sauce down too far.
- Leftovers keep well in the fridge for up to 3 days. The curry usually thickens on standing, so loosen it with a splash of water when reheating.
- If the chickpeas are older, they may need longer cooking after soaking. Cook until the centre is soft; timing alone is not a reliable guide.
