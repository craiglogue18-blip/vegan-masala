## Ingredients
- 440 g bread flour
- 2 tbsp sugar
- 7 g fast action dried yeast
- 2 tsp salt
- 200 ml warm (not hot) water
- 3 tbsp olive oil, divided
- 75 g ground almonds
- 25 g mixed dried fruit
- 25 g desiccated coconut
- 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped
- 3 tsp garlic paste (or 3 garlic cloves, minced)

## Method
1. In a large bowl, mix 440 g bread flour, 2 tbsp sugar, 7 g fast action dried yeast and 2 tsp salt.
2. Pour in 200 ml warm, not hot, water and mix until a rough dough forms.
3. Add 2 tbsp of the 3 tbsp olive oil and knead for about 10 minutes, until smooth and soft.
4. Brush a bowl with 1 tbsp of the remaining olive oil, place the dough inside, cover, and leave in a warm place for about 1 hour, until doubled in size.
5. Punch the dough down and knead briefly for 1 minute. Divide it into 6 equal pieces.
6. Mix 75 g ground almonds, 25 g mixed dried fruit, 25 g desiccated coconut and 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped, in a bowl.
7. Roll each piece into a ball, then flatten it into a disc about 10 to 15 cm across. Put a portion of filling in the centre, gather the edges over it, and seal tightly. Turn seam-side down.
8. Roll each stuffed piece gently into a teardrop shape about 5 mm thick, taking care not to split the dough.
9. Heat 1 tbsp of the remaining olive oil in a frying pan over medium heat. Cook each naan for about 5 minutes, turning once, until golden and puffed. Press it down lightly with a spatula if it balloons too much.
10. Warm the remaining 1 tbsp olive oil with 3 tsp garlic paste, or 3 garlic cloves, minced, and brush it over the hot naans before serving.

## Notes
- The dough should stay soft and a little tacky. If it fights the rolling pin, let it rest for a few minutes so the gluten relaxes.
- Keep the heat at medium. Too high and the outside colours before the centre cooks through.
- For a softer crust, stack the cooked naans under a clean tea towel while you finish the batch.
- Leftovers keep in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat in a dry pan or a low oven until the bread is supple again.
- The filling can be mixed ahead, but keep the dough covered so it does not dry out while it rests.
