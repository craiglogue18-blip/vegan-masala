## Ingredients
- 1 tsp olive oil
- 1 tsp mustard seeds
- 7 fresh curry leaves
- 1 tbsp chana dal
- 1/2 cup finely chopped onion
- 2 green chilies, slit
- 1/2 cup semolina
- 1 1/4 cups water
- salt to taste
- cilantro for garnish

## Method
1. Set a pan over medium heat and dry-roast 1/2 cup semolina for 5 to 8 minutes, stirring often, until it turns lightly golden and smells nutty. Tip it onto a plate so it does not keep browning.
2. Return the same pan to the heat and add 1 tsp olive oil. Add 1 tsp mustard seeds and 7 fresh curry leaves; when the seeds start to pop, stir in 1 tbsp chana dal.
3. Add 1/2 cup finely chopped onion and 2 slit green chilies. Cook until the onion softens and the chana dal takes on a little colour.
4. Pour in 1 1/4 cups water and add salt to taste. Bring it to a steady boil.
5. Lower the heat and stir in the roasted semolina in a steady stream, mixing as you go so it stays lump-free.
6. Cook for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring, until the water is absorbed and the upma looks soft but not wet. If it tightens too much, add a splash more water.
7. Cover the pan, turn off the heat, and let it rest for 1 to 2 minutes. Fluff with a spoon, then finish with cilantro for garnish.

## Notes
- Roasting the semolina first keeps the upma light and separate rather than sticky. Stop as soon as it smells nutty; deeper colour will make it taste dry.
- The finished texture should be soft and spoonable, not stiff. Add a little hot water if needed before serving.
- Leftovers thicken in the fridge. Reheat with a splash of water in a pan or covered in the microwave, then stir well.
- Serve it hot, while the tempering is still fragrant. A squeeze of lime sharpens the whole bowl, especially if the onions are mild.
